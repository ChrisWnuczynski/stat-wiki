# Stochastic process

Rank 309. Lighter tier. Concept page.

## Sources read

- Dodge (2008), printed pp. 521–522. A stochastic process is a family of random variables on one probability space. The entry uses the term when the index is infinite, defines a trajectory as $(X_t(\omega))_{t\in I}$, and states Markov, martingale, strict stationarity and independent-increment conditions.
- The page definition in the stochastic-process chapter allows an arbitrary index set $T$. That is wider than Dodge's "infinite index" sentence. Both were read; the article states the difference.

## Checked

- Two independent fair $\pm 1$ steps: $\mathbb P(X_2=0)=1/2$, $\mathbb P(X_2=\pm 2)=1/4$, variance $2$.
- $\mathbb P(X_1=1,X_2=0)=\mathbb P(X_2=2)=1/4$.
- A copied second step gives $X_2-X_1=X_1$, so the increments are not independent.
- Independent mean-zero, unit-variance steps still give mean $0$ and variance $2$ at time 2.

## Assumed

- The two steps are independent and each is fair. The variance addition uses that independence.

## Disputed

- Dodge reserves the name, in that entry, for an infinite index. The page definition allows a finite index. The worked example uses times $\{0,1,2\}$ and is labelled as a finite family.

## Not opened

- Doob (1953) and Grimmett and Stirzaker, cited in the source definition, were not reopened. New sentences cite Dodge.
