# Countable additivity

Rank 322. Lighter tier. Concept page.

## Sources read

- Durrett, *Probability: Theory and Examples*, 11 January 2019 text. Theorem 1.1.1, printed pp. 1–2: monotonicity, subadditivity, continuity from below, and continuity from above when the first set has finite measure. The proof of (iii) is the disjoint increment decomposition. Example 1.1.2, pp. 2–3, is a probability on a countable space given by summing point masses.
- Durrett's definition of a measure sets $\mu(\emptyset)=0$ directly. The page's axiom states countable additivity and $P(\Omega)=1$. The empty-set consequence is proved from that axiom rather than taken from Durrett's definition line.

## Checked

- Masses $(1/2)^k$ on $k\ge 1$ sum to $1$.
- $P(A_n)=1-2^{-n}$. $P(A_3)=0.875$, $P(A_4)=0.9375$, $P(A_5)=0.96875$.
- $P(\{4,5\})=3/32=0.09375=P(A_5)-P(A_3)$.
- Two disjoint events of probability $1/4$ have union probability $1/2$.

## Proved

Empty-set probability, and continuity from below by disjoint increments.
