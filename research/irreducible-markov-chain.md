# Irreducible Markov chain — source notes

- Checked: Levin and Peres, *Markov Chains and Mixing Times*, 2nd ed. (2017), author-hosted PDF, §§1.3 and 1.7: irreducibility is mutual accessibility of every state. The author-hosted text is at https://pages.uoregon.edu/dlevin/MARKOV/markovmixing.pdf. Publisher metadata and year checked against AMS ISBN 9781470429621.
- Checked: Levin and Peres, §1.5.3, Proposition 1.14: in a finite irreducible chain the stationary law can be built from expected occupation during a return cycle; its weight at a state is the reciprocal of mean return time. Their §4.3, Theorem 4.9 gives convergence for irreducible aperiodic finite chains. The cited convergence statement is conditional on aperiodicity.
- Checked: The original three-state illustration on this page was calculated exactly with SymPy. The matrix is row stochastic, has directed paths between every pair, has return routes of lengths 2 and 3 from state 1, and stationary row vector (1/3,4/9,2/9). Its printed matrix powers and arithmetic are covered by `checks/irreducible-markov-chain.py`.
- Checked: Dodge, *The Concise Encyclopedia of Statistics* (2008), pp. 330–331, entry “Markov, Andrei Andreevich,” attributes the early development of Markov chains to Markov's work and describes its historical connection to dependent random quantities. This is an orientation source for history, not a proof of the modern finite-chain theorem.
- Checked: The existing course chapter `sources/stat3004/chapters/sp-05-classes-and-reversibility.qmd`, §§“Communication and closed classes” and “When a stationary law fails to be unique,” supplies the page's pre-existing definitions, examples, and related links; retain those generated components. Its citations to Norris, Feller, and Grimmett–Stirzaker were inherited, not newly verified here.
- Assumed: The three states in the worked illustration represent successive system modes rather than empirical observations; all transition probabilities are hypothetical and explicitly introduced as such.
- Disputed: None. The statement “irreducible implies convergence” would be false without aperiodicity, as the two-state alternation counterexample shows.
