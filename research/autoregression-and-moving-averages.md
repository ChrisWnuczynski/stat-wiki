# Autoregression and moving averages — source notes

- Checked: Hyndman and Athanasopoulos, *Forecasting: Principles and Practice*, third edition, freely available author/publisher text at https://otexts.com/fpp3/arima.html, §§9.3–9.5. AR(p) uses lagged observations, MA(q) uses lagged innovations; a stationary AR(1) needs absolute coefficient below one; an MA(1) has a lag-one covariance but no covariance at larger lags; ARMA combines both. §9.4 explicitly distinguishes the MA stochastic model from moving-average smoothing.
- Checked: Dodge (2008), *The Concise Encyclopedia of Statistics*, printed p. 360, “Moving Average”, describes the smoothing use of the phrase. Printed p. 536, “Time Series”, traces historical time-series work through astronomical records and decomposition. Dodge's entry is not evidence for the stochastic MA(q) model.
- Checked: The inherited source `sources/book/chapters/12-time-series.qmd` contains the AR(1), random walk, MA(1), and three-series figure; all must remain. Its brief seasonal-temperature metaphor for a coefficient above one is only illustrative and does not give a stationary AR(1).
- Checked: For an AR(1) with mean 10, coefficient 3/5 and innovation variance 4, a last value 12 gives one-step forecast 56/5 = 11.2, two-step forecast 268/25 = 10.72 and stationary variance 25/4 = 6.25. For an MA(1) with coefficient 1/2 and innovation variance 4, variance is 5, lag-one covariance 2 and lag-one autocorrelation 2/5 = 0.4.
- Assumed: Worked numerical models have zero-mean uncorrelated innovations with constant variance; forecast examples condition on a known current value or innovation.
- Disputed: “Moving average” is ambiguous between an MA(q) innovation model and a rolling smoother. An AR coefficient equal to one is a random walk, not a weakly stationary AR(1). A sample ACF pattern does not by itself identify a unique ARMA order.
