# Maximum a posteriori estimate — source notes

- Checked: Gelman et al., *Bayesian Data Analysis*, third edition, author-hosted electronic edition at https://sites.stat.columbia.edu/gelman/book/BDA3.pdf, §2.3, printed pp. 32–33: posterior mean, median and mode summarize different aspects; the mode can be convenient computationally but a point estimate does not report uncertainty. In the uniform-prior binomial example, the posterior mean and interior mode differ.
- Checked: Hoff, *Bayes estimators* (2013), Duke author-hosted notes at https://www2.stat.duke.edu/~pdh10/Teaching/581/LectureNotes/Static/bayes.pdf, p. 11: beta–binomial posterior and posterior mean under squared-error loss.
- Checked: Tibshirani (1996), *Regression Shrinkage and Selection via the Lasso*, original JRSS B article, §5, p. 277, via the publisher DOI and the Stanford technical-report record. It derives lasso as a posterior mode under independent double-exponential coefficient priors. The already cited Hoerl–Kennard (1970), §2, establishes the ridge penalty; the Gaussian-prior derivation on the present page is algebraically direct.
- Checked: Dodge (2008), *The Concise Encyclopedia of Statistics*, printed p. 32, “Bayes, Thomas” and “Bayesian Statistics”: Price published Bayes's essay posthumously in 1763, and posterior density is proportional to prior times likelihood. This is background history, not a claim that Bayes introduced MAP terminology.
- Checked: Posterior for a Beta(3,2) prior and 6 successes in 8 Bernoulli trials is Beta(9,4), with interior mode 8/11, mean 9/13 and maximum-likelihood estimate 3/4. A Beta(2,2) density has mode 1/2 in probability coordinates but its odds transform has mode 1/3, confirming that continuous density modes depend on coordinates.
- Assumed: The binomial example represents conditionally independent trials with a common success probability; its prior is an explicit modeling choice.
- Disputed: MAP is not the probability of a single continuous parameter value, which is zero. A flat prior is coordinate-dependent, and a posterior mode is neither an invariant summary nor a general substitute for uncertainty intervals.
