# Extinction probability

Rank 349. Lighter tier. Concept page.

## Facts checked

- Durrett, fifth edition of 11 January 2019. Theorem 4.3.10, p. 231: if \(m<1\) then \(\mathrm{P}(Z_n>0)\le m^n\to 0\). Theorem 4.3.11, p. 231: if \(m=1\) and \(\mathrm{P}(\xi=1)<1\), extinction is certain; the proof uses that the integer martingale cannot settle at a positive size. Theorem 4.3.12, p. 232: if \(m>1\), the extinction probability is the unique root of \(\phi(s)=s\) in \([0,1)\), from conditioning on the first generation and from strict convexity.
- Quadratic \(p_0=1/4\), \(p_2=3/4\): mean \(3/2\), roots \(1/3\) and \(1\), and \(f(1/3)=1/3\).
- Subcritical \(p_0=p_1=1/2\): mean \(1/2\), only fixed point in \([0,1]\) is \(1\).
- Your turn: mean \(3/4\), roots \(1\) and \(2\).
- Check: mean \(1/8+3/2=13/8\). Pure doubleton: mean \(2\), extinction \(0\).
