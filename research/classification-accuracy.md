# Classification accuracy — source notes

- Checked: Bacallado and Taylor, Stanford STATS 202 notes (2022) at https://web.stanford.edu/class/stats202/notes/Classification/Evaluating-classifiers.html, “Evaluating a classification method”: empirical 0–1 error, confusion matrix, class-specific error rates, and the threshold trade-off. Their “Basic approach” page at https://web.stanford.edu/class/stats202/notes/Classification/Basic-approach.html states the Bayes classifier minimizes expected 0–1 error.
- Checked: James, Witten, Hastie and Tibshirani's official *Introduction to Statistical Learning* site links the freely downloadable second edition and author slides. The author-hosted chapter 5 slide deck at https://hastie.su.domains/ISLR2/Slides/Ch5_Resampling_Methods.pdf, slide 2, distinguishes training from test error and notes that training error can markedly underestimate test error.
- Checked: Dodge (2008), *The Concise Encyclopedia of Statistics*, printed p. 1, “Accuracy”, defines a broader estimator/measurement sense of accuracy. It does not define classification accuracy; only use it for the terminology distinction, never as support for a confusion-matrix formula.
- Checked: Existing `sources/stat3006/chapters/sl-09-error-rates.qmd` contains a three-class 150-case confusion-matrix example, class-specific rates, and held-out-versus-apparent error discussion. Preserve the generated article's inherited material and use a distinct binary, imbalanced example to make overall accuracy's limitation concrete.
- Checked: In a hypothetical 200-case test set with 20 positive and 180 negative cases, an all-negative rule has accuracy 180/200=0.90 and sensitivity zero. A rule with TP=15, FN=5, FP=20, TN=160 has accuracy 175/200=0.875, sensitivity 15/20=0.75, specificity 160/180=8/9, and precision 15/35=3/7. A lower-threshold rule with TP=18, FN=2, FP=36, TN=144 has accuracy 162/200=0.81, sensitivity 0.90, specificity 0.80, precision 1/3.
- Checked: The proposed separate bank question has sensitivity 0.80 and specificity 0.90. Its accuracy is 0.89 at positive prevalence 0.10 and 0.85 at prevalence 0.50, by the prevalence-weighted formula.
- Assumed: The illustrative test set is separate from model fitting and reflects the population where the classifier will be used. No class-dependent cost is specified in the simple accuracy calculation.
- Disputed: A high accuracy can arise from predicting only the majority class, can hide unequal error rates, and cannot by itself establish calibrated probabilities or adequate performance after a prevalence shift.
