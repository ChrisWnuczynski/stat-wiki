

## Lead context

A paper can be wrong in a way that no arithmetic error will reveal. The sample was whoever answered. The analysis was one of many that were tried. The published sentence treats a correlation as a cause, or a significant test as a large effect. Misuse of statistics is this family of failures: the numbers are real, and the conclusion drawn from them is not supported by the design or the analysis that produced them. Official agencies are explicitly entitled to object when their figures are read that way. Scientific literatures have a parallel problem when the path from data to a claimed finding is flexible.

## Definition

Misuse of statistics is the drawing of a conclusion that the sampling, the experiment, or the analysis does not warrant. It includes erroneous interpretation of a published official figure, which Principle 4 of the Fundamental Principles names directly: statistical agencies may comment on erroneous interpretation and misuse of statistics. It also includes practices that manufacture a statistically significant claim. Bias, in that second sense, is the combination of design, data, analysis, and presentation choices that produce a research finding when one should not have been produced. Selective or distorted reporting is the typical form. Chance error is not bias. A study can be honest and still be wrong because of sampling variation; misuse is the extra distortion that comes from how the study was built or how it was shown.{{cite:un_res68261}} (Principle 4){{cite:ioannidis2005}} (pp. 0696–0697)

The category is wider than fraud. Changing the definition of unemployment changes the official rate even when every form is counted correctly. An observational comparison can be numerically exact and still be the wrong basis for a causal sentence. A tiny p-value on a tiny effect can be reported as if it were a large one. Each of these is a misuse when the stronger claim is stated as though the method had established it.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 394){{cite:openintro2019}} (Ch. 1, §1.3.4)

## Statistical use

The tools that detect misuse are the same tools that do the job properly, applied to the claim rather than only to the table. A [confidence interval](confidence-interval.qmd) shows whether an effect is precisely estimated or merely non-zero. A [p-value](p-value.qmd) is a tail probability under a stated model, not a probability that the claim is true. Where many relationships are tested, the positive predictive value of a "significant" claim falls as the prior odds $R$ of a true relationship fall and as power falls. With a 0.05 threshold, $(1-\beta)R>0.05$ is the rough condition for a claim to be more likely true than false, before bias is added. Flexibility in outcomes and analyses acts like a bias proportion $u$ that moves non-findings into the reported column.{{cite:ioannidis2005}} (pp. 0696–0697)

In official series, the safeguard is documentation. Principle 3 requires sources and methods. A reader who cannot see how unemployment was defined cannot tell whether a drop is a labour-market change or a definitional one. Definitions of poverty, wellness, and occupational class are places where that choice is routine.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 394){{cite:un_res68261}} (Principle 3)

Observational studies support associations and hypotheses. They do not, by themselves, support the causal conclusion that would be reasonable from a randomised experiment. A [confounding variable](confounding-variable.qmd) is correlated with both the explanatory variable and the response. Sun exposure, in a comparison of sunscreen use and skin cancer, can produce an association that is not the effect of the sunscreen. Searching for confounders helps and does not finish the job, because an unmeasured one can remain.{{cite:openintro2019}} (Ch. 1, §1.3.4)



A numerical boundary keeps the definition attached to a calculation. List the integers from 1 through 18. Their total is 171, so their mean is 9.5. The first three integers have mean 2. The difference between the full-list mean and that three-integer mean is 7.5. The difference is a property of which units entered the average. It is not a property of the name of the field. A procedure that averages every eligible unit estimates the mean of that list. A procedure that averages only the first three estimates a different list. Misuse of statistics has to say which list is the target before the average is interpreted.

The same arithmetic limits a model fitted to the observed rows. The mean 9.5 describes the integers that were listed. Substituting a unit that was never on the list, or a unit that was excluded by the design, replaces the target. A [confidence interval](confidence-interval.qmd) for a mean is a statement about the estimator under a stated sampling model. A [standard error](standard-error.qmd) is the standard deviation of that estimator. Neither object repairs a list that was the wrong list. The procedures already named here include [confidence interval](confidence-interval.qmd), [p-value](p-value.qmd), [confounding variable](confounding-variable.qmd), [statistical literacy](statistical-literacy.qmd).

Two further limits are algebraic. The sum of deviations from the mean 9.5 is zero, so the mean is the least-squares centre of these 18 numbers. A robust centre, or a median, answers a different minimisation. Reporting the mean and calling it robust does not change the criterion that was minimised. And a second variable that moves in step with these integers can dominate a regression slope. The slope then describes the joint list, which is a larger object than the one-column mean 9.5.

## Connections

Misuse is the reason [statistical literacy](statistical-literacy.qmd) is taught as more than formula-reading, and it is part of the background to the [replication crisis](replication-crisis.qmd). [Sampling bias](sampling-bias.qmd), a convenience sample, and non-response are design failures that no later regression will honestly repair. [Multiple testing](multiple-testing.qmd) and the [Benjamini–Hochberg](benjamini-hochberg.qmd) procedure are the formal answer when many hypotheses are examined on purpose. A corrected analysis is not a confession that statistics cannot be used. It is a refusal to let a significant test, a dramatic chart, or an official percentage carry a conclusion the study did not earn.{{cite:openintro2019}} (Ch. 1, §1.3){{cite:ioannidis2005}} (p. 0696)

## History

The temptation to treat a count as a verdict is as old as official numbers. Political needs have repeatedly set the boundary of what official statistics study, expanding or limiting the contents. The twentieth-century argument about false discoveries is more specific. It treats a significant result as a claim that may be false even when the arithmetic is correct, and it lists the conditions that make that outcome likely: small samples, small effects, many tested relationships, flexible analysis, and interests that reward a positive report. Selective outcome reporting has been documented even for randomised trials, so the problem is not confined to obviously weak designs.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 394){{cite:ioannidis2005}} (pp. 0696–0698)
