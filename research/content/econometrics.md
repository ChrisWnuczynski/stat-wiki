## Lead context
A government wants to know what an extra year of schooling does to earnings, and it cannot assign years of school by lottery. Econometrics is the statistical study of economic models and economic data, built so that those non-experimental records can still be used for estimation, testing, and forecasting.{{cite:hansen2010}} (Ch. 1, §1.1, p. 1){{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 177)

## Definition — Scope
Ragnar Frisch's introduction to the first issue of *Econometrica* set the scope that is still used. Economic statistics alone, economic theory alone, and mathematics applied to economics alone each miss the point. Econometrics is the unification of the three: theoretical-quantitative reasoning and empirical-quantitative reasoning about economic problems. In present language, that is the joint study of economic models, mathematical statistics, and economic data.{{cite:hansen2010}} (Ch. 1, §1.1, p. 1)

Two branches sit inside that union. Econometric theory builds methods and studies their properties. Applied econometrics builds quantitative economic models and fits them to data. Because economic series are noisy and are rarely produced by an experiment, the models are stochastic. A deterministic equation with an error pasted on at the end is a poor match to that fact; the randomness belongs in the model from the start.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 177){{cite:hansen2010}} (Ch. 1, §1.2, p. 1)

## Statistical use
The everyday estimator is [least squares](least-squares.qmd), in [simple](simple-linear-regression.qmd) and [multiple linear regression](multiple-linear-regression.qmd). With an intercept and the right covariates, the fitted coefficient is the slope of the linear projection. Omitting a relevant regressor folds that variable into the error, so the remaining coefficients estimate a different projection from the one the investigator named.{{cite:hansen2010}} (Ch. 4, §4.12)

Three data shapes organise the dependence. A cross-section has one row per person, household, or firm, and independence across rows is the usual working assumption. A [time series](time-series.qmd) is indexed by time, so successive observations are dependent and a random-sample variance is the wrong one; the [autocorrelation](autocorrelation-function.qmd) of residuals is then a substantive feature, not a nuisance to ignore. A panel follows the same individuals over time: individuals may be treated as independent of one another while repeated measurements on one individual are not. Heteroskedasticity, serial correlation, and [multicollinearity](multicollinearity.qmd) change the [standard errors](standard-error.qmd) even when the point estimator stays the same.{{cite:hansen2010}} (Ch. 1, §§1.4–1.5, pp. 3–4)

Causal reading is a separate step. Most economic data are observational. A positive association between schooling and wages is compatible with a return to school and also with the fact that higher ability produces both more school and higher wages. An [observational study](observational-study.qmd) can identify a conditional association; a causal claim needs a design or a set of assumptions that says which variables are held fixed. Forecasting, by contrast, can be useful even when that causal step is left open, which is why simultaneous-equation and time-series forecasts became a large part of the subject.{{cite:hansen2010}} (Ch. 1, §1.4, p. 3){{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 177)

## History
William Petty's political arithmetic in the late seventeenth century is an early argument that economic questions should be put in numerical form. Léon Walras's general-equilibrium theory later supplied a mathematical skeleton for an economy of many markets. On 29 December 1930 a group of economists, statisticians, and mathematicians founded the Econometric Society in Cleveland and, in January 1933, the journal *Econometrica*. Frisch was its first editor. Trygve Haavelmo's 1944 essay on the probability approach argued that quantitative economic models are probability models, and that estimation and testing then belong to mathematical statistics. Simultaneous equations for forecasting became one of the pieces that made the field look distinct from applied statistics at large.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 177){{cite:hansen2010}} (Ch. 1, §§1.1–1.2, p. 1)

## Connections
The linear-model core is shared with statistics: [ordinary least squares](ordinary-least-squares.qmd), the [Gauss–Markov](least-squares-estimators-are-unbiased.qmd) question of unbiasedness, and [maximum likelihood](maximum-likelihood.qmd) when a full distribution is specified. [Demography](demography.qmd) and national accounts supply many of the series. [Epidemiology](epidemiology.qmd) faces the same observational-data problem in another subject matter. What is distinctive here is the combination of an economic model, a dependence structure that is often temporal or hierarchical, and data that the investigator did not assign.{{cite:hansen2010}} (Ch. 1, §§1.4–1.5, pp. 3–4)
