## Lead context

A government wants to know what an extra year of schooling does to earnings, and it cannot assign years of school by lottery. Econometrics is the application of statistical methods to economic data. Economists use those methods to test theories and to make forecasts. Because the records are not produced by an experiment, and because they contain randomness, the models are stochastic rather than deterministic equations with an error pasted on at the end.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 177)

The same data often come in clusters. Households share a village, workers share an industry, and a panel follows the same person or firm across years. Errors can be correlated inside a cluster and treated as uncorrelated across clusters. A point estimate can then look precise under a formula that assumes independent rows, while a cluster-robust standard error shows a wider interval. Both the coefficient and its standard error enter the report: an asymptotic 95 percent interval is the estimate plus or minus 1.96 times the standard error.{{cite:cameron_miller2015}} (§I)

## Definition

Econometrics concerns the application of statistical methods to economic data, for testing economic theories and for forecasting. The Econometric Society was founded in Cleveland on 29 December 1930 by economists, statisticians, and mathematicians, to promote research into the mathematical and statistical theories associated with economics. The society's journal *Econometrica* appeared for the first time in January 1933. The development of simultaneous-equation models that could be used on time series for economic forecasts is the point at which the subject emerged as a field distinct from applied statistics at large, and those forecasting models remain part of it.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 177)

Two working branches sit inside that description. One builds and studies methods: least squares, instrumental variables, the generalised method of moments, and the standard errors that match the dependence in the data. The other builds a quantitative economic model and fits it. The method is not a substitute for the model. A regression of wages on schooling estimates the coefficient in the equation that was written down. If the error is correlated with schooling, that coefficient is not the return the ministry asked for, and a different instrument, or a different equation, is a different target.{{cite:cameron_miller2015}} (§I; §VII.B)

## Interpretation

A fitted coefficient answers the equation that was estimated, under the moment condition that equation uses. Ordinary least squares of $y$ on $X$ is inconsistent for a structural slope when $\E[u\mid X]\ne 0$. An instrument vector $Z$ changes the condition to $\E[u\mid Z]=0$, together with a rank condition that $Z$ actually moves $X$. In the just-identified case, where $Z$ and $X$ have the same number of columns, the instrumental-variable estimator is $(Z'X)^{-1}Z'y$. When $Z$ is longer than $X$, two-stage least squares and the linear generalised method of moments use a weight matrix to combine the extra moments. The efficient two-step weight is the inverse of a consistent estimator of the variance of those moments, and with clustered errors that variance is summed at the cluster level. The weight is defined only when there are more clusters than instruments.{{cite:cameron_miller2015}} (§VII.B, §VII.B.3)

Dependence changes the interval even when the point estimator stays the same. In a cross-section, the usual working assumption is independence across people, households, or firms, and clustering on village or state is the departure from it. In a panel, repeated measurements on one individual can be correlated while different individuals are treated as independent. A difference-in-differences study that follows states over years is the same structure with the state as the cluster. Ignoring the within-cluster correlation makes the default standard error too small and the test too willing to reject. The cluster-robust variance is the one the asymptotics license when the number of clusters grows and the errors are uncorrelated across clusters.{{cite:cameron_miller2015}} (§I; §VII)

Forecasting does not require the coefficient to be a causal return. A simultaneous-equation system fitted to a time series can be used to project the series, and that use is part of why the field separated from general applied statistics. A causal reading is a further claim. It needs the instrument condition, or some other design that says which variables are held fixed. Schooling and wages can move together because school raises wages and also because ability produces both. The regression reports the association in the equation that was fit. The causal sentence needs its own assumption, stated in the same place as the estimate.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 177){{cite:cameron_miller2015}} (§VII.B)

A time-series regression makes the dependence visible in the residuals. Dodge's highway-accident series, accidents per million miles in the United States from 1970 to 1984, is fit by a simple line, $4.59-0.141\,t$, with $t=0$ in 1970. The Durbin–Watson statistic on those residuals is $d=0.53$. For a regression with one explanatory variable the 5 percent bounds quoted for that calculation are $d_L=1.08$ and $d_U=1.36$. Because $d<d_L$, the test rejects independent errors in favour of positive first-order autocorrelation. The slope can still be computed. The independent-error standard error cannot.{{cite:dodge2008}} (pp. 174–175)

## Statistical use

The everyday estimator is [least squares](least-squares.qmd), in [simple](simple-linear-regression.qmd) and [multiple linear regression](multiple-linear-regression.qmd). With an intercept and the covariates that define the projection, the fitted coefficient is the slope of that projection. Omitting a relevant regressor folds it into the error, so the remaining coefficients estimate a different projection. [Ordinary least squares](ordinary-least-squares.qmd) is the computational core. When a regressor is endogenous, the instrumental-variable and two-stage least-squares estimators replace it, and the cluster-robust variance replaces $(Z'X)^{-1}$ times a residual outer product summed over clusters. Over-identified models can use the linear generalised method of moments, which is more efficient than two-stage least squares when $\E[uu'\mid Z]$ is not a scalar matrix. The over-identifying-restrictions test, in the clustered case, has to be computed from the cluster version of the two-step estimator.{{cite:cameron_miller2015}} (§VII.B, §VII.B.3)

Three data shapes organise the dependence. A cross-section has one row per person, household, or firm. A [time series](time-series.qmd) is indexed by time, so successive observations are dependent and a random-sample variance is the wrong one. The [autocorrelation](autocorrelation-function.qmd) of residuals is then a feature of the series, as in the accident regression, not a nuisance that the table can ignore. A panel follows the same individuals over time. Heteroskedasticity, serial correlation, and [multicollinearity](multicollinearity.qmd) change the [standard errors](standard-error.qmd) even when the point estimator is left as it is. Weak instruments are a separate failure: if the first-stage relationship is faint, the instrumental-variable estimator can stay biased in large samples and the Wald statistic need not be approximately normal. A common screen is a first-stage $F$ statistic, with a rule of thumb that $F<10$ signals weakness, but that rule was developed for independent errors. With clustered errors the $F$ statistic itself has to be cluster-robust, and the independent-error critical values are no longer the ones the screen was built on.{{cite:cameron_miller2015}} (§VII.B.2){{cite:dodge2008}} (pp. 174–175, 177)

Nonlinear models add a further choice of target. A population-averaged logit keeps $\E[y\mid x]=\Lambda(x'\beta)$ after any within-cluster correlation has been averaged out, and a generalised estimating equation can insert a working correlation such as equicorrelation. A random-effects logit puts a cluster intercept inside the probability, and the coefficient in that model is not the population-averaged coefficient. The two numbers answer different questions and should not be set side by side as if one were a correction of the other.{{cite:cameron_miller2015}} (§VII.C)

## History

The advantages of putting economic questions in mathematical and statistical form were argued early. In the later seventeenth century William Petty's political arithmetic set out a numerical approach to economic description. Léon Walras's general-equilibrium theory later supplied a mathematical account of an economy of many markets, and that theory is the skeleton later writers treated as a basis for modern econometrics. The institutional start is dated precisely: the Econometric Society, Cleveland, 29 December 1930, and *Econometrica* from January 1933. Simultaneous equations for time-series forecasts then gave the subject a body of models that applied statistics was not already organised around.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 177)

Later practice made the sampling variation of those fits as much a part of the work as the point estimate. Cluster-robust inference is the form that practice takes when the economic units are groups: villages, industries, firms followed over time, or states in a difference-in-differences design. The point estimate can be ordinary least squares, instrumental variables, or a moment estimator. The standard error has to add up the dependence inside the group rather than treat every row as a fresh draw.{{cite:cameron_miller2015}} (§I; §VII)

## Connections

The linear-model core is shared with statistics: [ordinary least squares](ordinary-least-squares.qmd), the [Gauss–Markov](least-squares-estimators-are-unbiased.qmd) question of unbiasedness under a stated second-moment condition, and [maximum likelihood](maximum-likelihood.qmd) when a full distribution is specified. [Estimating equations](estimating-equations.qmd) are the moment restrictions that instrumental variables and the generalised method of moments solve. [Demography](demography.qmd) and national accounts supply many of the series that enter an economic model. [Epidemiology](epidemiology.qmd) faces the same observational-data problem in another subject matter: an association is not yet the effect of an intervention nobody assigned. What is distinctive here is the combination of an economic equation, a dependence structure that is often temporal or grouped, and data that the investigator did not assign.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 177){{cite:cameron_miller2015}} (§I; §VII.C)
