## Lead context
Some treatments are handed out by a policy, a price, or a distance, rather than by an investigator with a randomisation list. A natural experiment is a comparison that uses that outside assignment as if it separated people into treated and untreated groups. {{cite:hernan_robins2020}} (p. 211) {{cite:openintro2019}} (p. 32)

## Motivating example
The effect of smoking cessation on weight cannot be read off the people who quit, because they differ from the people who do not. Cigarette price is a candidate for an outside push: a higher price may change the decision to quit, and the claim, if it can be sustained, is that price changes weight only through that decision and shares no causes with weight. A threshold such as a state price above or below \(\$1.50\) turns the push into a binary contrast. In one such split the chance of quitting was \(25.8\%\) on the high-price side and \(19.5\%\) on the low-price side. {{cite:hernan_robins2020}} (p. 211)

## Intuition
An investigator's coin flip is an experiment because the investigator assigns the treatment. {{cite:openintro2019}} (p. 32) A natural experiment keeps the logic of a contrast between groups and drops the investigator. Something else, already operating in the world, sorts people. The sorting is useful only when it meets the conditions that a real assignment would have met: it changes the treatment, it does not change the outcome by another route, and it is not itself confounded with the outcome. {{cite:hernan_robins2020}} (p. 209) {{cite:hernan_robins2020}} (p. 211)

## Definition
A natural experiment is an observational comparison in which the treatment contrast is generated by a process outside the study, and that process is proposed as an instrument or as a stand-in for assignment. Access is one such process: distance or travel time to a facility, calendar period when a treatment's availability changes, or the price of the treatment. {{cite:hernan_robins2020}} (p. 211) The data remain observational. No treatment was explicitly applied or withheld by the investigator. {{cite:openintro2019}} (p. 25)

The outside factor \(Z\) is only a candidate. Of the three instrumental conditions, the association between \(Z\) and treatment can be examined. The absence of any other path from \(Z\) to the outcome, and the absence of common causes of \(Z\) and the outcome, are assumptions. {{cite:hernan_robins2020}} (p. 211)

## Properties
The observed association between \(Z\) and the outcome is not the effect of the treatment. It is the effect of the outside factor, which is smaller than the treatment effect when the outside factor only partly changes treatment. The ratio of that association to the association between \(Z\) and treatment is the usual instrumental-variable estimand. {{cite:hernan_robins2020}} (p. 213)

A second pattern uses a comparison group that never received the outside change, together with measurements from before and after. The extra change in the affected group is a [difference in differences](difference-in-differences.qmd), and it equals the effect in the treated only under additive equi-confounding. {{cite:hernan_robins2020}} (p. 106)

Because the investigator did not randomise, scientific randomisation is not what protects the contrast. An arbitrary or merely systematic split is a different assignment from a random one. {{cite:nist_glossary2002}} (Glossary, "randomization, scientific")

## Statistical use
Natural experiments are analysed as observational studies that hope to borrow the identifying power of an instrument or of a pre-treatment comparison series. Distance to a treatment facility and calendar time are typical access instruments. {{cite:hernan_robins2020}} (p. 211) The resulting estimate is a causal effect only under the conditions named for that method, which the data do not confirm. {{cite:hernan_robins2020}} (p. 211) {{cite:openintro2019}} (p. 25) A designed randomised experiment remains the design in which those conditions are created rather than argued. {{cite:openintro2019}} (p. 32)

## Worked example
A bridge closure sends 4 recorded trips by the long route, with times 30, 40, 50, and 40 minutes, and 4 trips by the short route, with times 20, 25, 30, and 25 minutes. The investigator did not choose the route. The long-route mean is \(160/4 = 40\). The short-route mean is \(100/4 = 25\). The contrast is \(40 - 25 = 15\) minutes. That number is the association of route with time. It estimates a causal effect of route only if the closure's sorting satisfies the conditions used to interpret it. {{cite:hernan_robins2020}} (p. 211) {{cite:openintro2019}} (p. 25)

**Your turn.** One more short-route trip takes 20 minutes. What are the new short-route mean and the new contrast with the long-route mean of 40?

::: {.callout-tip collapse="true" title="Answer"}
The short-route times sum to 120 over 5 trips, so the mean is 24. The contrast is \(40 - 24 = 16\).
:::

## Common misconceptions
Calling a comparison natural does not make it randomised. The outside factor can be associated with the outcome for reasons that have nothing to do with the treatment, and then the contrast is confounded. {{cite:hernan_robins2020}} (p. 211) {{cite:openintro2019}} (p. 25)

A large association between the outside factor and the treatment is not the treatment effect. In the cigarette-price split the quit rates were \(25.8\%\) and \(19.5\%\), a gap of about six percentage points, which is the denominator of an instrumental-variable ratio, not the effect of quitting on weight. {{cite:hernan_robins2020}} (p. 211) {{cite:hernan_robins2020}} (p. 213)

## Assumptions and limits
The exclusion restriction and the absence of common causes of the outside factor and the outcome are not guaranteed by the story that makes the factor plausible. They are unverifiable in the same way that "all confounders were measured" is unverifiable. {{cite:hernan_robins2020}} (p. 211) {{cite:hernan_robins2020}} (p. 104) A weak push, a denominator near zero, leaves the ratio too noisy to use. {{cite:hernan_robins2020}} (p. 213)

## Connections
The outside factor is an [instrumental variable](instrumental-variable.qmd) when the three instrumental conditions hold. The before-and-after version is [difference in differences](difference-in-differences.qmd). [Causality](causality.qmd) is the potential-outcome contrast the design hopes to estimate. A [designed experiment](designed-experiment.qmd) creates the assignment instead of finding it. The untreated side of the contrast is a [control group](control-group.qmd) only in the loose sense of a reference; it was not withheld by protocol.

## History
Treating price, distance, and calendar time as substitutes for assignment is a development of instrumental-variable work in observational studies. {{cite:hernan_robins2020}} (p. 211) The insistence that a causal connection is the business of a randomised experiment, in which researchers assign treatments, is the contrasting tradition in introductory design. {{cite:openintro2019}} (p. 32)
