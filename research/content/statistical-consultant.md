## Lead context

Someone asks a statistician to design a survey, to say what a messy data set can support, or to explain why a significant test is not the decision. That person may be called a consultant, an analyst, or a collaborator. The work is the same activity the ethical guidelines call statistical practice: designing collection, summarising, processing, analysing, interpreting, and presenting data, and developing or deploying models and algorithms. The client relationship adds a duty to say what the analysis cannot do, and to resist pressure to produce a result that was wanted before the data were examined. The product is the design and the interpretation. The client's product, whether a drug, a census table, or a factory process, is a different object.{{cite:asa_ethical2022}} (Purpose){{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 155)

## Definition

A statistical consultant is a practitioner who advises a client on the design, analysis, or interpretation of data. The American Statistical Association's guidelines use a wider term, statistical practitioner, precisely so that the role is not confined to people whose job title is consultant. Throughout the guidelines, the practitioner includes all who engage in statistical practice, regardless of job title, profession, level, or field of degree. Practice includes designing the collection of data, summarising, processing, analysing, interpreting, and presenting it, and developing or deploying models and algorithms. The guidelines are written for individuals and are also relevant to organisations that do this work. A consultant in a university, a government agency, or a firm is inside that definition. So is a researcher doing the same tasks without the title. The guidelines aim to promote accountability by telling the people who rely on the work what standards they should expect. Society, the text says, benefits from informed judgments supported by ethical statistical practice.{{cite:asa_ethical2022}} (Purpose)

The scope is the advice and the analysis, not the client's subject matter. Deming's consulting after 1946 was statistical studies, and in Japan it was sampling methods and management ideas carried to industry. He had a doctorate in physics, a decade at the Department of Agriculture, and from 1939 a post at the Census Bureau, where he started sample censuses that were later copied elsewhere. The client's product was not the statistician's product. The statistician's product was the design and the interpretation. That split is the definition of the role in any later setting: a medical trial, a household survey, a production line. The consultant is responsible for the statistical part of the decision, including the part that says the data do not support the decision the client hoped for.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 155)

## Interpretation

The ethical reading of the role is specific, and it is easy to thin out into a slogan. Professional integrity and accountability require taking responsibility for one's work. Ethical practice supports valid and prudent decision-making with appropriate methodology. The practitioner represents capabilities and activities honestly and treats others with respect. The guidelines then list conducts that integrity rules out. The practitioner does not knowingly conduct practices that exploit vulnerable populations or create or perpetuate unfair outcomes. The practitioner opposes efforts to predetermine or influence the results, and resists pressure to interpret data selectively. Those sentences are the interpretation of a consulting engagement in which the client arrives wanting a significant result. The service being requested, in that case, is not the service the role provides.{{cite:asa_ethical2022}} (Professional integrity)

A second principle covers the data and the methods. The practitioner seeks to understand and mitigate known or suspected limitations, defects, or biases in the data or the methods, and communicates potential impacts on the interpretation, the conclusions, the recommendations, the decisions, or the other results. Transparency covers the assumptions, the methods, the limitations, the possible sources of error, and algorithmic biases. It also covers the difference between objectives stated before the data were seen and objectives formed afterwards, and it covers the disclosure of multiple comparisons and of any adjustment. The interpretation a consultant owes the client is therefore not only the estimate. It is the estimate together with the list of things that would change it. An analysis that hides a confounding variable, a sampling bias, or a fragile model fails that duty even when the arithmetic is correct.{{cite:asa_ethical2022}} (Integrity of data and methods)

Stakeholders are the people who fund, contribute to, use, or are affected by the work. The practitioner seeks to establish what they hope to obtain, and tries to obtain enough subject-matter knowledge to make the practice meaningful. Regardless of personal or institutional interests, or of external pressure, the practitioner does not use statistical practices to mislead a stakeholder. Stakeholders are told about limitations on use and reuse, and about scope, cost, and precision. When interests conflict, scientific integrity and the guidelines take priority. That is the interpretation of independence inside a contract: the contract does not purchase a predetermined conclusion.{{cite:asa_ethical2022}} (Responsibilities to stakeholders)

## Statistical use

The consultant's technical work is whatever the question requires. A sample design, a randomised experiment, a regression, and a control chart are four of the usual instruments. Each has a condition the advice has to keep visible. A sample design needs a frame that matches the population named in the question, and a known chance of selection if a sampling error is to be calculated. A randomised experiment needs the treatment assigned, the other conditions controlled or balanced, and a comparison that did not receive the experimental treatment. A regression needs a stated mean function and a stated loss, and a residual plot that asks whether the mean function left a pattern behind. A control chart needs limits built from the in-control process, and an action that follows an out-of-control signal rather than an adjustment made because a point moved inside the limits. The ethical constraint is the same in all four: limitations are communicated, and the method is appropriate to the decision.{{cite:asa_ethical2022}} (Purpose){{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 155){{cite:openintro2019}} (Ch. 1, §1.3.5, §1.4.1)

A consulting engagement therefore has two outputs. One is an estimate, a test, a design, or a chart. The other is a statement of what would change the conclusion. The second output is part of the analysis. A numerical illustration is small enough to show the split. A client has six treated changes, 8, 10, 6, 12, 9 and 9, summing to 54, and six comparison changes, 2, 4, 3, 3, 5 and 1, summing to 18. The means are 9 and 3, and the contrast is 6. The first output is the contrast. The second output is the list of conditions under which 6 estimates a treatment effect: the assignment was random, the other conditions were held in common, the comparison did not receive the treatment, and the six-plus-six sample is the population the decision is about. If any of those conditions fails, the arithmetic 9 minus 3 is still correct and the interpretation changes. Sample size is part of the same advice. The guidelines expect an informed recommendation on sample size and on methodology, so that a study does not use too many subjects or too few, and does not expose subjects to needless risk. A design that cannot estimate the effect the client named is a recommendation against the study, not a small print caveat after the study has been run.{{cite:asa_ethical2022}} (Responsibilities to research subjects){{cite:openintro2019}} (Ch. 1, §1.4.1)

The same split applies when the data already exist. Secondary records, a register, a billing file, or a previous agency's survey can be analysed, and the consultant's job is to say which population those records cover. Coverage error, non-response, and a definition that changed halfway through a series are limitations of the data. They are reported with the estimate. Reusing a file does not assign a treatment, and the guidelines' demand to distinguish planned analyses from analyses suggested by the results applies with particular force to a file that was not collected for the new question.{{cite:nist_glossary2002}} (Glossary, "coverage error"){{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 152){{cite:asa_ethical2022}} (Integrity of data and methods)

## History

Consulting as a named career sits beside academic statistics from early in the twentieth century. Deming's path is a documented one. He was born in 1900 in Sioux City, Iowa, studied at Wyoming and Colorado, and received a doctorate in physics from Yale in 1928. After the doctorate he worked for ten years in the laboratory of the Department of Agriculture. In 1939 he moved to the Census Bureau and there started sample censuses that later provided an example for similar censuses elsewhere. In 1946 he left the bureau to become a consultant in statistical studies and a professor of statistics at New York University. In 1947 he went to Tokyo as a consultant on sampling. From 1950, Japanese industry adopted his management teaching. For a long time, up to 1980, American companies ignored the theories. He died in Washington in 1993. The episode is a reminder that the consultant's influence can be institutional, through a census method or a factory system, and not only a single client's data set. The chart that many of those factories later used is older than the consulting tour: Shewhart's memorandum of 16 May 1924 sketched a control chart at Bell Laboratories, and his 1931 book set the pattern for process control. Sampling inspection, developed there by Dodge and Romig, is a sister method. Deming carried statistical control into industrial management. The chart itself is Shewhart's device.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 155){{cite:nist_pmc11_2002}} (§6.1.1)

The ethical guidelines approved by the ASA Board of Directors on 1 February 2022 are the current professional statement of what anyone in that role owes the people who rely on the work. They sit on top of a longer practice in which the title has varied and the duties have been argued in particular cases. The guidelines' choice of the word practitioner, rather than consultant alone, is a historical correction: the work was never confined to people whose contracts used one job title.{{cite:asa_ethical2022}} (Purpose)

## Connections

Consulting is how [medical statistics](medical-statistics.qmd), [official statistics](official-statistics.qmd), and [quality control](quality-control.qmd) often meet a subject-matter specialist who is not a statistician. It is also where [misuse of statistics](misuse-of-statistics.qmd) is either interrupted or laundered. A client who wants a significant result is asking for a different service from a client who wants to know what the study can support. The guidelines' answer is that the practitioner resists pressure to interpret data selectively and does not use the practices to mislead a stakeholder. The statistical answer is the same: the design and the uncertainty stay visible in the advice. [Sampling](sampling.qmd), a [designed experiment](designed-experiment.qmd), and [statistical process control](statistical-process-control.qmd) are three of the techniques the advice has to match to the question. [Statistical literacy](statistical-literacy.qmd) is the competence a client needs in order to read the second output, the statement of what the analysis cannot do. A consultant who omits that output has left the client with a number and without the conditions.{{cite:asa_ethical2022}} (Purpose, Professional integrity){{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 155)
