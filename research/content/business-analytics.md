## Lead context

A firm that records every sale still has to decide what to stock, what to charge, and which customers to contact. Business analytics is the use of data, statistical and quantitative analysis, and explanatory or predictive models to drive those decisions. The activity is older than the phrase. Operations research and management science already applied probability, statistics, optimisation, and simulation to decisions in firms. The phrase spread with the argument that some firms compete by making that use routine, across many decisions, rather than by commissioning a single specialist study.

## Definition

Davenport describes organisations that have built their businesses on collecting, analysing, and acting on data. The examples he uses are firms that apply quantitative analysis across a wide range of activities, not in one function only: pricing, inventory, customer offers, and the measurement of what employees contribute. The distinguishing feature is coordination. The analysis is part of a strategy pushed to decision makers at every level, and the people who decide are equipped with quantitative tools. A firm may know what products its customers want. An analytics competitor, in this account, also estimates what prices those customers will pay, how many items each will buy, and which triggers increase the purchase.{{cite:davenport2006}} (pp. 1–2)

Vidgen, Shaw, and Grant adopt a definition they attribute to Davenport and Harris: the extensive use of data, statistical and quantitative analysis, explanatory and predictive models, and fact-based management to drive decisions and actions. They stress the actionable part. A model that is not used to change a decision has not completed the activity the definition names. They also record a shorter operations-research formulation, current in 2016, of analytics as the scientific process of transforming data into insight for better decisions. The two wordings agree on the sequence from data to a decision. They differ in how hard they press the organisational claim that the insight has to be acted on.{{cite:vidgen2017}} (§1)

Three kinds of question sit inside that sequence, and they use different statistical tools. Descriptive work summarises what the files contain: tables, averages, and segments of customers or products. Predictive work estimates what is likely on a new case, by [regression](least-squares.qmd), classification, or another model scored on cases the model did not see. Prescriptive work takes a prediction into a choice: an inventory level, a price, or an offer. Davenport's account of inventory and of perfect orders is the prescriptive end. A prediction of demand that is not allowed to change the order quantity has stopped at the predictive step.{{cite:davenport2006}} (pp. 1–2){{cite:vidgen2017}} (§1)

## Interpretation

The output of a predictive model is a number attached to a case: a probability of purchase, an expected demand, a score. The interpretation that makes it analytics, rather than a statistical exercise left in a report, is the decision rule that uses the number. A threshold on a score determines who is contacted. An expected demand determines an order. The statistical properties of the score, bias and variance and calibration, matter because they change those decisions. A score that ranks customers well and is badly calibrated can still be the wrong input to a price.

Vidgen, Shaw, and Grant treat the path from data to value as an organisational system, not as a property of the algorithm. They describe a capability that sits between the data the organisation holds and the value it can take from actions based on better decisions. The capability includes analysts, data scientists, and information-technology staff, aligned with the business strategy. Their reading of the management literature is that becoming data-driven is not a technical installation. Leadership, skills, the decision process, and the technology all have to move. A model deployed into a decision process that nobody is allowed to change does not create the value the definition talks about.{{cite:vidgen2017}} (§1)

Machine learning adds pattern-finding that does not start from a subject-matter equation. That is useful for a large binary table of purchases or for a text field in a complaint log. It is not a substitute for a designed collection of data. A large observational file can still be the wrong file for the decision, because the customers who appear in it are the ones who already bought, or the prices in it are the ones the firm already charged. The statistical distinction between a prediction and a causal effect of changing the price remains in force inside a firm. Davenport's analytics competitors are described as using models to choose actions. The justification for reading a coefficient as the effect of an action is a design question, the same one a randomised test of an offer is meant to answer.{{cite:davenport2006}} (p. 2){{cite:vidgen2017}} (§1)

## Statistical use

Descriptive work is the reporting of what the files contain. Averages, rates, and segments are [data analysis](data-analysis.qmd) in the ordinary sense. They answer "what happened." They do not by themselves answer "what will happen if we change the offer."

Predictive work is regression, classification, and other models scored on new cases. The check is [cross-validation](cross-validation.qmd) or a held-out period: the cases used to fit the model are not the cases used to score it. A model that predicts last quarter's sales on last quarter's data, and is then judged on those same sales, has not been tested as a prediction. Davenport describes predictive modelling as one of the tools analytics competitors apply to customer behaviour and to operations. The statistical content of that tool is a sampling model for a new case, a loss that says which errors matter, and a holdout that estimates the loss.{{cite:davenport2006}} (p. 2)

Prescriptive work takes the prediction into an optimisation. An inventory level balances the cost of holding stock against the cost of a missed sale. A price balances margin against the probability of purchase. The optimisation is only as good as the prediction that enters it, and the prediction is only as good as the data and the model. Vidgen, Shaw, and Grant list the management obstacles that sit around this technical core. In the Delphi study they report, the challenges practitioners ranked highest were managing data quality, using analytics for improved decision making, creating a strategy for data and analytics, the availability of data, and building data skills. Data quality is a statistical issue: a model fit to a file with systematic errors in the prices or the customer identifiers estimates the wrong conditional distribution. Skills and strategy are the conditions under which the estimate is allowed to change a decision.{{cite:vidgen2017}} (§4.3)

The ethical constraint is part of the same use. Vidgen, Shaw, and Grant treat data and information ethics as one of the focal challenges, alongside strategy and people. A score that ranks customers can be accurate and still be a score the firm should not act on, if the features are proxies for a protected characteristic or if the data were collected for a different purpose. The statistical report of accuracy does not settle that question. It is one input to it.{{cite:vidgen2017}} (§1)

## History

Operations research and management science are the older professional homes of quantitative business decisions. They already included optimisation, simulation, probability, and statistics aimed at a decision in a complex system. The phrase business analytics spread with Davenport's 2006 argument that a small set of firms compete on this quantitative analysis as a strategy, rather than as a project. The reprint lays out characteristics of those firms: enterprise-wide use, leadership support, and a supply of people who can work with the numbers.{{cite:davenport2006}} (pp. 1–2)

Empirical work on managers then treated the obstacle as organisational. Vidgen, Shaw, and Grant's 2017 study combined a Delphi ranking by practitioners with interviews in three organisations. The ranking and the interviews agreed that strategy, people, and the decision process were at least as pressing as the choice of algorithm. Their ecosystem diagram puts an analytics capability between data and value. That is a management claim with a statistical consequence: the value of a model is realised only in the decisions it changes, so an evaluation that stops at predictive accuracy on a holdout has measured one piece of the activity.{{cite:vidgen2017}} (§1, §4.3)

## Connections

[Econometrics](econometrics.qmd) is the parallel activity when the decisions are macroeconomic or the data are markets and households in the economist's sense. [Data science](data-science.qmd) overlaps the predictive and computing parts: the same regression, the same holdout, the same concern with a file that does not fit the decision. [Engineering statistics](engineering-statistics.qmd) overlaps the prescriptive part when the decision is a process setting rather than a customer offer. Official and scientific [data sharing](data-sharing.qmd) is a different release problem. Business analytics is the use of a file the organisation already holds, to change a decision inside that organisation. The statistical standards that apply, a sampling model, a holdout, and a distinction between prediction and the effect of an intervention, are the same standards as in the rest of statistics.{{cite:vidgen2017}} (§1){{cite:davenport2006}} (p. 2)
