## Lead context

Numbers do not become evidence by being written down. Someone has to decide what is counted, collect it, and turn the collection into a summary another person can use. A statistician is that person, whether the numbers are a national census, a laboratory assay, or a sample drawn to stand in for a larger population. The work runs in two directions. Upstream, data are either found, as by-products of administration, or made, by a sample or an experiment designed for the question. Downstream, the same numbers are described, compared, and used to say something about people or units that were not measured one by one. A statistician may do only part of that chain. The chain is still the occupation: a contribution to the delivery of numerical information that someone else can use.{{cite:okrasa_witek2013}} (p. 320){{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 518)

## Motivating example

Four districts report 120, 80, 200, and 100 births in a year. The four figures are not yet a description of the country. Their total is 500, the district mean is 125, and the largest district accounts for $200/500=0.4$ of the births. Producing those three summaries, and saying which territory and which year they refer to, is already statistical work. So is refusing to add a fifth figure that counted births in a different way.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 518){{cite:okrasa_witek2013}} (p. 320)

## Definition

A statistician develops or applies statistical theory and methods in order to collect, organise, interpret, and summarise numerical data so that the result is usable information. That occupational description, as reported from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics classification, covers mathematical and survey statisticians. It leaves out, as a separate occupation, the person whose main task is only to design questionnaires or run a field team, though those tasks often sit in the same office.{{cite:okrasa_witek2013}} (p. 320)

A broader official-statistics reading counts as a statistician anyone who contributes to getting statistics and data into the hands of users. Under that reading the work splits into the theoretical statistician, the survey methodologist, the subject-matter specialist, the information technologist, and the survey manager. The common point is the delivered product, not a single university degree.{{cite:okrasa_witek2013}} (p. 320)

In the narrower disciplinary sense, statistics is the study of numerical data and of the techniques for learning from data that are incomplete. Descriptive techniques put the data into a form that can be read. Inferential techniques use a sample to say something about the population the sample came from. A person who does either, as a practice rather than as a one-off sum, is doing the work the name picks out.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 518)

## Interpretation

The occupation is not confined to one faculty. The same methods are used in industrial production, environmental measurement, medicine, and public agencies. A working list of areas runs through probability and statistical theory themselves, biology and biometry, commerce and inventories, demography, the measurement of production and prices, education, engineering and quality, health and insurance, marketing, management, psychology, the social sciences, and the natural sciences generally. The list is a map of employers.{{cite:dodge2008}} (pp. 518–519)

That spread makes the occupation heterogeneous. Statistics is used inside other sciences, so people trained in those sciences apply statistical methods without always carrying the job title. Occupational classifications have counted on the order of two hundred detailed titles under the broader heading. A panel that asked who should unambiguously be called a statistician was raising a boundary question. The panel did not produce one roster.{{cite:okrasa_witek2013}} (pp. 319–320)

Two products should be kept apart. A [statistic](statistic.qmd) is a number computed from a sample by a rule that does not contain an unknown parameter. A statistician is the person who chooses the rule, the design that makes the rule meaningful, and the words in which the number is reported. The mean of the four district counts is a statistic. The decision that these four districts, and not a different geography, are the right totals is a decision of the person.

In a statistical agency the delivery can be a census table, a price index, or a sample estimate with a stated margin. In a laboratory it can be the design of an experiment and the analysis that belongs to that design. In a firm it can be an inventory or a market study. The common standard is that another user can tell what was counted, how it was counted, and what conclusion the count will and will not support.{{cite:okrasa_witek2013}} (p. 320){{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 518)

A person who has computed one classroom mean, or who quotes a published figure, has not thereby taken up the occupation. The occupation is the practice of producing usable numerical information, including the design of the collection.{{cite:okrasa_witek2013}} (p. 320) The name is also not reserved for the mathematical end of the work. Survey managers and subject-matter specialists are inside the broader definition when they contribute to the statistics that users receive. The definitions are about tasks, not about a licence. There is no single educational path that the occupation requires, which is why a discussion of who counts as a statistician turns on competencies and on the product rather than on the name of a degree.{{cite:okrasa_witek2013}} (pp. 319–320)

Quality control, a census, and a designed biological experiment share tools and do not share a single inferential target. A statistician who moves from one to another has to restate what the numbers are for. The techniques for incomplete data apply where a full enumeration was not done. They are the wrong tool when the user needed the complete list.{{cite:dodge2008}} (pp. 518–519)

## Worked example

The district births illustrate the smallest complete report. The observations are $120, 80, 200, 100$. Then

$$
120+80+200+100=500, \qquad \frac{500}{4}=125, \qquad \frac{200}{500}=0.4.
$$


The smallest share is $80/500=0.16$. The range of district counts is $200-80=120$, which is larger than the mean. A report that gives only $0.4$ has dropped the total and the definition of a district. A report that gives only $500$ has dropped the inequality across districts. A summary that mentioned only the mean would hide a spread of that size. The three numbers together are a description. An inference to next year's births would be a further claim, and it needs a model the four figures do not contain.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 518)

**Your turn.** Three districts report 30, 45, and 75 births under the same definition. Find the total, the mean, and the share in the largest district.

::: {.callout-tip collapse="true" title="Answer"}
The total is $30+45+75=150$. The mean is $150/3=50$. The largest share is $75/150=0.5$.
:::

## Connections

The number that the work produces from a sample is a [statistic](statistic.qmd). The plan that assigns treatments before those numbers are observed is a [designed experiment](designed-experiment.qmd). A count of many units at one time is [cross-sectional data](cross-sectional-data.qmd). A count of the same units at several times is [panel data](panel-data.qmd). The [First International Statistical Congress](first-international-statistical-congress.qmd) was a meeting of people doing this work for states, trying to make their documents comparable. A [game of chance](game-of-chance.qmd) is the small random experiment from which the probability calculations began.

## History

Counting people and goods is ancient. Statisticians in the modern sense appear in the late seventeenth century, with the modern state and with the administrative needs of France and England. Through the eighteenth century the activity was still often described as political arithmetic, the application of numerical calculation to questions of state. The nineteenth century then built the methods and the distributions that experimental science and the study of populations both used, and it built the national offices whose staff are the public face of the occupation.{{cite:dodge2008}} (pp. 393, 518)

By the twentieth century the diversity of methods had made statistics a discipline of its own, with specialised encyclopedias, at the same time as the job title spread across the sciences on the list above. Contemporary discussions of the profession still start from the same boundary problem: the discipline is a body of methods, and the occupation is a large and mixed set of people who get numerical information to users.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 518){{cite:okrasa_witek2013}} (pp. 319–320)
