

## Lead context

A process engineer who changes one temperature and watches a yield is already running an experiment, whether or not a statistician is in the room. Engineering statistics is the use of experimental design, process comparison, and reliability methods to make those decisions when the units are products, materials, or manufacturing steps.{{cite:nist_engineer_design2002}} (§5.3, "Choosing an experimental design")

## Definition

The designs an engineer is expected to know are the designs used across engineering and scientific work. They include completely randomised designs, [randomised blocks](randomised-block-design.qmd), Latin squares, full and fractional factorials, screening designs, and response-surface designs for second-order surfaces. The sequence is practical: state the objective, choose the process variables and their levels, then choose the design that can estimate the effects the objective cares about.{{cite:nist_engineer_design2002}} (§5.3, "Choosing an experimental design")

Alongside design sits the comparison of processes that are already running. Tests and [confidence intervals](confidence-interval.qmd) are used to decide whether a mean has shifted, whether two processes differ, and whether a trend or an outlier is present. Reliability methods treat lifetime data, including units that have not failed by the end of the test. The common setting is a measurement that will be used to change a process, a specification, or a test plan.{{cite:nist_process_intro2002}} (§7.1, "Introduction"){{cite:nist_censoring2002}} (§8.1.3.1, "Censoring")

[Biostatistics](biostatistics.qmd) uses the same factorials and survival models on patients rather than on components. [Econometrics](econometrics.qmd) shares regression and is much less often able to assign the factors. Reliability theory is the probability side of the lifetime calculations. Quality control and process monitoring are the sequential cousins: they ask whether a stable process has left the state the design assumed. The shared rule is that the data come from a process someone can change, and the analysis is built so that the change can be read off the design.{{cite:cleveland2001}} (p. 22){{cite:nist_process_intro2002}} (§7.1, "Introduction")

## Statistical use

A [designed experiment](designed-experiment.qmd) assigns the factor combinations. In a full factorial every combination appears, so main effects and interactions can be separated. A fractional factorial drops some combinations and accepts that certain effects are aliased, which is useful when many factors might matter and only a few runs are affordable. Response-surface designs, including central composite and Box–Behnken layouts, estimate a curved surface in a few continuous factors so that a yield can be moved toward a maximum. Blocking removes a known nuisance, such as a batch of raw material or a day, from the comparison of the factors.{{cite:nist_engineer_design2002}} (§5.3, "Choosing an experimental design")

Once the data exist, [least squares](least-squares.qmd) fits the mean model, and an [analysis of variance](anova-sum-of-squares-decomposition.qmd) splits the variation into factors, blocks, and residual. [Hypothesis tests](ingredients-of-a-hypothesis-test.qmd) and intervals then say whether a change is large relative to the run-to-run noise. For lifetimes, a fixed test length produces Type I [censoring](censoring-statistics.qmd): the failure times that fall before the cutoff are observed, and the survivors contribute the information that their lifetimes exceed the cutoff. A [Weibull](weibull-distribution.qmd) or lognormal model, or a distribution-free [survival](survival-function.qmd) curve, turns those records into a failure probability.{{cite:nist_censoring2002}} (§8.1.3.1, "Censoring"){{cite:nist_process_intro2002}} (§7.1, "Introduction")



A numerical boundary keeps the definition attached to a calculation. List the integers from 1 through 14. Their total is 105, so their mean is 7.5. The first three integers have mean 2. The difference between the full-list mean and that three-integer mean is 5.5. The difference is a property of which units entered the average. It is not a property of the name of the field. A procedure that averages every eligible unit estimates the mean of that list. A procedure that averages only the first three estimates a different list. Engineering statistics has to say which list is the target before the average is interpreted.

The same arithmetic limits a model fitted to the observed rows. The mean 7.5 describes the integers that were listed. Substituting a unit that was never on the list, or a unit that was excluded by the design, replaces the target. A [confidence interval](confidence-interval.qmd) for a mean is a statement about the estimator under a stated sampling model. A [standard error](standard-error.qmd) is the standard deviation of that estimator. Neither object repairs a list that was the wrong list. The procedures already named here include [randomised blocks](randomised-block-design.qmd), [confidence intervals](confidence-interval.qmd), [Biostatistics](biostatistics.qmd), [Econometrics](econometrics.qmd).

Two further limits are algebraic. The sum of deviations from the mean 7.5 is zero, so the mean is the least-squares centre of these 14 numbers. A robust centre, or a median, answers a different minimisation. Reporting the mean and calling it robust does not change the criterion that was minimised. And a second variable that moves in step with these integers can dominate a regression slope. The slope then describes the joint list, which is a larger object than the one-column mean 7.5.



The integer list used above is a device for keeping the target fixed. With 14 integers the mean is determined by their total, and any smaller subset has a different mean. A field that reports the smaller mean as if it were the mean of the full list has changed the target. The same distinction applies when the units are people, firms, or plots rather than integers: the eligible list is part of the definition of the quantity. A model fitted inside the list can be a good description of those units and a poor description of a larger population. Saying which population is intended is a modelling step, not a decoration added after the fit. Neighbour procedures remain useful only for the target they were built for. A confidence interval inherits the sampling model. A regression slope inherits the conditional-mean model. A survival probability inherits the rule for censored times. None of those procedures chooses the eligible list.
## History

Several standard methods were built on engineering and industrial data. John Tukey’s numerical spectrum analysis grew out of physical-science and engineering series. George Box’s response-surface methods grew out of chemical-process experiments, where the goal was to move a set of controllable factors toward a better yield. R. A. Fisher’s designs, developed with agricultural researchers and then taken into industrial standardisation, supplied the factorial and blocking language that process engineers still use. The NIST/SEMATECH handbook collects those designs and the process comparisons in the form a working engineer consults.{{cite:cleveland2001}} (p. 22){{cite:nist_engineer_design2002}} (§5.3, "Choosing an experimental design")
