## Lead context
Dual optimisation searches for the strongest lower bound generated by nonnegative multipliers on inequality constraints.

## Motivating example
A constrained optimisation problem can be difficult in its parameter variables but easier in multipliers attached to its constraints. For a support-vector classifier, eliminating the normal vector leaves an objective involving inner products between observations. This both supplies an alternative optimisation problem and explains how kernel evaluations enter the method.{{cite:cortes_vapnik1995}} (§§2–3)

## Definition — From a Lagrangian to a bound
Consider minimising $f_0(x)$ subject to $f_i(x)\leq0$ and $h_j(x)=0$. Its Lagrangian is
$$
L(x,\lambda,\nu)=f_0(x)+\sum_i\lambda_i f_i(x)+\sum_j\nu_jh_j(x),
$$
with $\lambda_i\geq0$. The dual function is $g(\lambda,\nu)=\inf_x L(x,\lambda,\nu)$, where the infimum respects the functions' domains. For any feasible $x$, the inequality terms are nonpositive and the equality terms vanish. Therefore $g(\lambda,\nu)\leq f_0(x)$. Maximising this bound over the allowed multipliers is the Lagrange dual problem.{{cite:boyd_vandenberghe2004}} (§§5.1.1–5.1.3, 5.2.1)

[]{#property-slater-strong-duality}

The dual function is concave because it is a pointwise infimum of affine functions of the multipliers. This property holds even when the primal problem is nonconvex. Strong duality means that the best dual bound equals the primal optimum; it needs suitable conditions and does not follow merely from writing down the Lagrangian. For a convex problem, Slater's constraint qualification is one sufficient condition.{{cite:boyd_vandenberghe2004}} (§§5.1.2, 5.2.3)

## Properties — Eliminating the classifier's parameters
For the soft-margin objective $\|w\|^2/2+C\sum_i\xi_i$, constrain $y_i(w^\top x_i+b)\geq1-\xi_i$ and $\xi_i\geq0$. Use multipliers $\alpha_i,\mu_i\geq0$. Stationarity in $w,b,\xi_i$ gives
$$
w=\sum_i\alpha_i y_i x_i,\qquad
\sum_i\alpha_i y_i=0,\qquad
C-\alpha_i-\mu_i=0.
$$
Thus $0\leq\alpha_i\leq C$, and substitution yields
$$
\max_\alpha\left\{\sum_i\alpha_i-
\frac12\sum_{i,j}\alpha_i\alpha_j y_i y_j x_i^\top x_j\right\},
\quad \sum_i\alpha_i y_i=0,\quad0\leq\alpha_i\leq C.
$$
The upper bound comes from nonnegative slack multipliers; it is part of the soft-margin dual.{{cite:cortes_vapnik1995}} (pp. 293–295, Appendix A.2)

A positive semidefinite Gram matrix makes the negative quadratic term concave in $\alpha$. Replacing an inner product by a kernel is valid when it is an inner product in a feature representation, preserving this structure. The multipliers then determine a classifier through kernel values against the training observations.{{cite:cortes_vapnik1995}} (§§3–4)

## Worked example
First minimise $x^2$ subject to $x\geq1$. Write the constraint as $1-x\leq0$. For $\lambda\geq0$,
$$
g(\lambda)=\inf_x\{x^2+\lambda(1-x)\}
=\lambda-\lambda^2/4,
$$
since the inner minimum occurs at $x=\lambda/2$. The maximum occurs at $\lambda=2$ and equals one, matching the primal solution $x=1$. A feasible multiplier such as $\lambda=1$ instead gives the valid but weaker lower bound $3/4$.{{cite:boyd_vandenberghe2004}} (§§5.1–5.2)

For a classifier with observations $x_1=-1,y_1=-1$ and $x_2=1,y_2=1$, the equality constraint gives $\alpha_1=\alpha_2=a$. The dual objective reduces to $2a-2a^2$. With $C\geq1/2$, its optimum is $a=1/2$, giving $w=1$, $b=0$, and primal and dual objective values $1/2$. With $C=1/4$, the optimum is $a=1/4$ and $w=1/2$. Choosing $b=0$ gives two slacks of $1/2$, and both objective values are $3/8$.{{cite:cortes_vapnik1995}} (pp. 293–295, Appendix A.2)

**Your turn.** Replace the scalar constraint $x\geq1$ by $x\geq2$. Find the dual optimum and the primal minimum.

::: {.callout-tip collapse="true" title="Answer"}
The dual function is $2\lambda-\lambda^2/4$, maximised at $\lambda=4$ with value four. Its inner minimiser is $x=2$, which is feasible and has primal value $x^2=4$.
:::

## Connections — The optimised variable can change interpretation
Duality can also exchange estimation targets. In sparse Gaussian precision estimation, the primal variable is a precision matrix, while its dual can be expressed in a covariance matrix constrained to remain within an entrywise band around the empirical covariance. A dual algorithm therefore need not be directly updating the parameter named in the primal objective.{{cite:banerjee_elghaoui_daspremont2008}} (§2.2)
