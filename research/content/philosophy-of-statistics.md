## Lead context
Statistical practice produces numbers and then treats them as answers. The philosophy of statistics is the set of questions about what those answers mean: what is being inferred, what is assumed in order to infer it, and what is deliberately left unsaid about causes. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 518) {{cite:hernan_robins2020}} (p. vii)

## Definition
Philosophy of statistics, as it appears inside statistical work rather than as a separate metaphysical subject, is the account of how incomplete data can support a statement that goes beyond the data, and of which assumptions that step requires.

The discipline itself is defined as a scientific system for collecting, organising, analysing, and interpreting numerical data, and for obtaining knowledge from incomplete data. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 518) Descriptive techniques stay with the observed information. Inferential techniques generalise it to a population. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 518) That generalisation is the step with a philosophical cost: a sample supports a sentence about a population only under a story about how the sample was drawn. {{cite:openintro2019}} (p. 22) {{cite:openintro2019}} (p. 25)

One division is between procedures that do and do not put a distribution on the unknown parameter before the data are seen. A Bayesian analysis has four moves. Name the observations. Name a parametric family for the way they were generated. Express uncertainty about the parameter by an initial distribution. Calculate the final distribution of the parameter given the data. The first two moves are shared with statistical inference generally. The third, the prior distribution, is the move that is not. It is the difficult one when little is known about the parameter beforehand, and the proposed remedy is a reference distribution chosen to represent that missing information. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 33)

A second division is between association and causation. An observational comparison shows how outcomes differ across groups that already differ in exposure. A causal contrast compares potential outcomes under two actions in the same population. {{cite:openintro2019}} (p. 25) {{cite:hernan_robins2020}} (p. 5) One can refuse metaphysical claims about what a cause "is" and still insist that an effect is a numerical contrast under interventions, such as the risk of death with a transplant and without one. That refusal is a philosophical position: stay with identification and estimation, and do not pretend the data speak without assumptions. {{cite:hernan_robins2020}} (p. vii) {{cite:hernan_robins2020}} (p. 3)

## Statistical use
The shared skeleton of a parametric analysis is a family of distributions for the data. Estimation, intervals, and tests are operations inside that family. Adding a prior turns the unknown parameter into something that itself has a distribution, and the output is a final distribution rather than a single preferred estimate. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 33)

The causal skeleton is different. A randomised experiment assigns the treatment so that a later difference can be tied to the assignment. {{cite:openintro2019}} (p. 32) An observational analysis that wants the same kind of conclusion has to declare the assumptions that replace assignment. Methods that avoid measuring every common cause, including difference in differences and instrumental variables, do not avoid assumptions. They change which unverifiable assumption is being used. {{cite:hernan_robins2020}} (p. 104) {{cite:hernan_robins2020}} (p. 106) A model, statistical or probabilistic, predicts either a measured quantity or a relative frequency. It does not, by being written down, justify a causal reading. {{cite:nist_glossary2002}} (Glossary, "model") {{cite:openintro2019}} (p. 305)

## History
The name of the subject comes from the state's need to count, and Kendall's dating puts an early use of the word in 1589 and the origin of modern statistics near 1660. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 518) The Bayesian organisation, with an explicit initial distribution updated to a final one, is the form associated with Bayes' theorem and developed as a full inferential position in the twentieth century. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 33) The insistence that causal inference state both the question and the assumptions, and that it not collapse into a metaphysical theory of cause, is the position of the interventionist account of effects. {{cite:hernan_robins2020}} (p. vii) {{cite:hernan_robins2020}} (p. 3)
