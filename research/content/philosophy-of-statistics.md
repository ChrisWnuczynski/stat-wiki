## Lead context
Philosophy of statistics is the set of questions about what a statistical conclusion assumes and what it is entitled to mean. A numerical summary, a probability, a model, and a causal sentence can all be printed from the same table. They are not the same kind of sentence. The philosophy of the subject asks which assumptions were used to pass from the table to the sentence, and which further assumptions would be needed before the sentence could be used as a guide to action. The questions are not a separate science from statistics. They are the questions that decide what a given statistical method has established.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 518){{cite:gelman_bda2013}} (pp. 11–12)

## Definition
Three clusters cover the questions that ordinary statistical work actually raises.

The first cluster is the meaning of a probability. A probability is a number attached to an event. The mathematical definition requires the number to be nonnegative, to add over mutually exclusive events, and to give the whole sample space the value $1$. That definition does not choose among a ratio of equally likely cases, a limit of relative frequencies in repeated trials, and a degree of belief about a single case. Gelman records the symmetry argument and the frequency argument as two justifications that both still require a judgement, about the physical arrangement and about the way the repetitions are comparable. A conclusion that quotes a probability has to say which justification it is using. A coverage frequency of a procedure across repeated samples is not, by that frequency, the probability that a parameter lies in one computed interval.{{cite:gelman_bda2013}} (pp. 11–12){{cite:dodge2008}} (pp. 430–432)

The second cluster is the meaning of a model. Dodge describes statistics, in the modern sense, as a discipline concerned with numeric data and with techniques for obtaining knowledge from incomplete data. A model, in the engineering glossary, is a mathematical statement of the relations among variables. It may have a statistical part, which predicts a measured quantity, and a probability part, which predicts the relative frequency of random outcomes. Fitting the model estimates the statement. It does not turn the statement into a description of every mechanism that produced the numbers. A conclusion that says "the slope is $1.2$" has estimated a coefficient inside a named model. A conclusion that says "the slope is the effect of the predictor" has added a claim about interventions that the coefficient, by itself, does not contain.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 518){{cite:nist_glossary2002}} (Glossary, "model")

The third cluster is the meaning of a comparison. An experiment applies a treatment, or withholds it, and a control group supplies the reference. An observational study records variables without that explicit application. OpenIntro's distinction is that causal conclusions from experiments are often reasonable, while the same conclusions from observational data are treacherous. A confounding variable is correlated with both the explanatory variable and the response, and it can produce an association that reverses the effect one would have expected. Sunscreen use and skin cancer can move together because sun exposure moves both, even if sunscreen reduces cancer risk. The fitted association is a fact about the records. The causal sentence is a further claim, and it needs the temporality and the design that the association does not supply.{{cite:openintro2019}} (Ch. 1, §1.3.4){{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 62)

## Properties
The three clusters share a structure. Each one separates a number the method computes from a sentence the number is sometimes asked to support. The axioms fix the arithmetic of probability and leave the interpretation open. The model fixes a relation among variables and leaves the mechanism open. The association fixes a comparison in the sample and leaves the intervention open. A careful report names the number and names the extra assumption that would promote it to the stronger sentence. Dropping the extra assumption is not a weaker form of the same sentence. It is a different sentence.

The clusters also constrain each other. A posterior probability uses the degree-of-belief reading for the unknown and a sampling model for the data. Reporting it as a long-run error rate of a test uses the frequency reading for a procedure. The two reports can be computed from the same data and can point in different directions, because they are answers to different questions. Likewise, a large association in an observational study can be estimated precisely, in the sense of a small standard error, and still be the wrong causal comparison, because precision is a property of the estimator under the model and confounding is a property of the design. Precision does not buy the causal sentence.{{cite:gelman_bda2013}} (pp. 11–12){{cite:openintro2019}} (Ch. 1, §1.3.4)

A further property is that the assumptions are checkable only in part. The axioms will detect an assignment that contradicts itself, for example nonnegative weights that do not add to one. They will not detect a symmetry judgement about a bent coin. Residual plots will detect a bend that a straight line missed. They will not detect a confounder that was never recorded. The philosophy of a method is, in part, a list of the failures that the method's own checks cannot see. Those failures are design conditions: no unmeasured common cause, repetitions that really are comparable, a model that really does contain the relation being estimated.{{cite:dodge2008}} (pp. 430–432){{cite:openintro2019}} (Ch. 1, §1.3.4)

Statistics, as Dodge divides the subject, already contains the split these questions turn on. Descriptive statistics represents information in a usable form. Inferential statistics generalises that information from samples to populations. A descriptive sentence about the sample can be exact. An inferential sentence about a population is entitled only to what the sampling and the model support. Treating the descriptive sentence as if it were the inferential one, or the inferential one as if it were a causal one, is the confusion the three clusters are meant to block.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 518)

## History
The probability questions are the oldest. Games of chance produced the counting rules, and Pascal and Fermat turned a gambler's interrupted game into a mathematical problem. A frequency account, tying the number to repeated trials, grew up beside the counting account. The axiomatic account then stated the rules without choosing the story that supplied the numbers. Extending the numbers to an unknown chance, given the data, is the step Bayes's essay takes, and it is the step that forces the interpretation question into the open: the probability of a parameter is not a frequency of the parameter unless a further story says so.{{cite:dodge2008}} (pp. 430–432)

The word statistics, Dodge notes, comes from the Latin for the state, and it was used for the counts a government needs: population, births, trade. In the modern sense it is a discipline of numeric data and of techniques for incomplete data, with a descriptive part and an inferential part. The applications Dodge lists, from biology and medicine to psychology and the social sciences, are places where the same numerical techniques meet different entitlements. A scale of measurement in psychology and a mortality comparison in medicine can share a mean and a standard error. What the mean is entitled to mean depends on the design, which is why the philosophy of the subject is not settled by the formula.{{cite:dodge2008}} (pp. 518–519)

The causal question has its own line. Epidemiology's comparison of risks is a probability comparison, and the strength of an association is not enough to establish a cause: the supposed cause has to precede the effect, among other conditions. OpenIntro's teaching version of the same point is the observational study, in which no treatment was applied and a confounder can manufacture the association. The philosophical content of that point is stable across the two sources. An association is a property of the joint records. A cause is a claim about what would change if the exposure were changed.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 62){{cite:openintro2019}} (Ch. 1, §1.3.4)
