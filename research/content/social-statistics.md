## Lead context

A society is not only an economy and not only a population count. It is employment, schooling, households, crime, and political participation, measured so that groups can be compared. Social statistics is that measurement. It uses the sampling and modelling toolkit of any other empirical science, and it draws many of its totals from the official system. The distinctive problems are the definitions, which are argued over, and the designs, which rarely look like a simple random sample of independent people. A percentage of unemployment, a rate of incarceration, and a mean years of schooling are social statistics only together with the definitions that produced them and the frames from which the people were drawn.{{cite:dodge2008}} (pp. 394, 519)

## Definition

Social statistics is the application of statistical methods to the structure and behaviour of human societies. In a survey of the uses of statistics, the social-science entry is the construction of sampling techniques and of tests concerning social systems and well-being, together with the analysis of cultural differences in values and behaviour. The official list of domains makes the content concrete. Population and households cover families, births, adoptions, deaths, marriages, divorces, and migrations. Employment covers companies, jobs, and working conditions. Education covers primary, secondary, and higher schooling, and the research that sits beside them. Health covers mortality, morbidity, workers, equipment, and cost. The legal system covers courts and recidivism. Politics covers elections and participation. Those series are social statistics when the object is a social fact, whether or not the agency that publishes them also publishes the national accounts.{{cite:dodge2008}} (pp. 394, 519)

The Fundamental Principles describe the charge in one sentence. Official statistics serve government, the economy, and the public with data on the economic, demographic, social, and environmental situation. The social clause is one of the four situations the principles say a democratic information system has to cover. The statistics that meet a test of practical utility are to be compiled and made available on an impartial basis, by official statistical agencies, to honour a public entitlement to information. Principle 3 adds the condition under which that information can be read: agencies present sources, methods, and procedures according to scientific standards, so that a correct interpretation is possible. Principle 5 allows the numbers to come from surveys or from administrative records, chosen with regard to quality, timeliness, cost, and the burden on respondents. A social series can therefore be a sample survey of households or a reuse of a register of births, and both routes are inside the official mandate.{{cite:un_res68261}} (Principles 1, 3, 5)

Psychology's measurement of individuals is a neighbour, not the same subject. Dodge's survey of uses keeps it separate: scales of intelligence, personality, and normal or abnormal behaviour of an individual, rather than the sampling of social systems. A survey of voters and a scale of a personality trait can use the same regression and still answer different questions. Demography, listed beside the social sciences, studies the increase of a population through births, deaths, and migration, and the personal, social, and economic structure of that population. Social statistics borrows those counts and then asks about the institutions and the behaviour that the counts sit inside.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 519)

The unit is usually a person, a household, or an area that contains people. The variable is often a category: employed or not, a level of schooling, a type of household, a recorded offence. Continuous measurements appear as well, as income, age, or a test score, and they are summarised with the same means and dispersions used anywhere else. What makes the summary a piece of social statistics is the population it refers to and the definition that decided who counted as unemployed, poor, or enrolled. A change in that definition moves the series even when behaviour does not. History, Dodge notes, shows the mutual dependence of statistics and the state, and that dependence is why the limits of official statistics have been hard to draw: political power expands or restricts the contents according to its needs.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 394)

## Statistical use

Collection is the first statistical problem. A census enumerates the units in the frame. Between censuses, samples do the work. Simple random sampling gives every subset of the chosen size the same probability, which is the design whose error formulas are taught first. People are not scattered as independent draws from a list. They live in areas, attend institutions, and share households, so stratified and cluster designs are the ones that match the way a social population is grouped. Stratification can use region, age, or a socioprofessional class as the control variable, the sort of variable Dodge lists for a stratified student sample. A convenience sample, or a survey that misses the people who do not respond, describes a subpopulation that may not be the one named in the table. Non-response changes who is in the data. Coverage error is the different failure in which the frame itself never listed the units the conclusion names. Almost all of the usual estimates assume a random framework. Without it, the printed standard error does not describe the uncertainty of the estimate.{{cite:openintro2019}} (Ch. 1, §1.3.1, §1.3.5){{cite:dodge2008}} (pp. 152, 497, 525){{cite:nist_glossary2002}} (Glossary, "coverage error")

Analysis then uses the ordinary models. Contingency tables and tests of independence summarise categorical social facts, such as the joint distribution of housing tenure and application type. Regression describes how a numerical outcome moves with several factors at once. Observational studies are the usual design, because the investigator does not assign employment, schooling, or a criminal record. An observational comparison supports an association. It does not, on its own, support a causal sentence, because a confounding variable can move both the supposed cause and the outcome. Repeated surveys of the same definition are a time series, and a break in the definition is a break in that series. Official indicators, pursued since the 1960s in economics, health, and education, were built so that politicians could see the effects of economic, social, and educational policies. The indicator is only as stable as the definition and the design behind it.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 394){{cite:openintro2019}} (Ch. 1, §1.3.4)

A worked numerical pattern shows why the design has to travel with the percentage. Suppose four districts report 120, 80, 200, and 100 births in a year. The total is 500 and the mean is 125. The largest district's share is \(200/500=0.4\). Those four numbers describe the districts that reported. They describe a wider region only if the missing districts are argued for, and they describe a change in fertility only if the definition of a live birth and the completeness of registration stayed fixed. The arithmetic is the small part. The social statistic is the arithmetic together with that argument.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 152){{cite:un_res68261}} (Principle 3)

## Common misconceptions

A social percentage is not a fact about behaviour until the definition and the frame are stated. Two countries can publish unemployment rates that differ because one counts a person seeking work and the other counts a person receiving a benefit. The numerical gap is then a gap in classification, and a model fitted to the two series will treat a coding difference as if it were a difference in labour markets. Principle 3's demand for sources and methods is the official answer to that misreading. Publishing the percentage alone is how a social number is turned into a slogan.{{cite:un_res68261}} (Principle 3){{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 394)

A large official sample is not a remedy for a frame that never listed the people the headline names. The 1936 telephone prediction that missed the electorate is the sampling illustration of the same point: the list was of households with telephones, and the electorate was larger. Non-response has the same shape inside a social survey. If the people who do not answer differ, on the variable being measured, from the people who do, the answered subset is a different population from the one that was drawn. Convenience samples of students, of clinic patients, or of online volunteers are samples of those groups. They are not samples of a national population merely because the questionnaire used national wording.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 469){{cite:openintro2019}} (Ch. 1, §1.3.5){{cite:nist_glossary2002}} (Glossary, "convenience sample")

An association in a social cross-section is not a causal effect of the variable that was written on the left of a regression. Schooling and income move together, and so do many other facts about the same people. The observational warning in a first course applies directly: sunscreen use and skin cancer can move together because sun exposure moves both, and the coefficient on sunscreen is then the wrong causal sentence. Social statistics is full of that pattern. A coefficient is an estimate of the association the model stated. The conditions under which it would be an effect are further conditions, and the equation does not state them.{{cite:openintro2019}} (Ch. 1, §1.3.4)

Individual measurement is not social measurement. A scale of intelligence or of personality, in the sense Dodge separates under psychology, answers a question about a person. A sampling design for a social system answers a question about a population of people. Using one as a substitute for the other changes the target. A mean test score in a school is a social statistic when the school, the test, and the enrolled population are the object. The same number, read as a measurement of one child's ability, is a different claim.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 519)

## Connections

Social statistics shares its sampling frame with [official statistics](official-statistics.qmd) and its concern with populations with [public health](public-health.qmd) and [demography](demography.qmd). [Psychological statistics](psychological-statistics.qmd) is the individual-measurement neighbour. [Secondary data](secondary-data.qmd) are the registers and administrative records Principle 5 allows an agency to reuse. Principle 4 gives statistical agencies a role that matches that concern: they are entitled to comment on erroneous interpretation and misuse.{{cite:un_res68261}} (Principles 1, 4, 5){{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 519)

## History

The quantitative study of populations is older than the phrase social statistics. Censuses are nearly as old as organised states. Kings of Babylon and Egypt, and Chinese, Greek, Hebrew, Persian, and Roman rulers, counted soldiers and goods. A position for an official statistician is recorded for the Chow dynasty, and Tacitus reports an Augustan census of soldiers, ships, and wealth. The word statistics is traced to the Latin *status*, the state, which already says that the subject grew up as an administrative practice. Sporadic counts go back more than 6000 years. What changed later was the attempt to make the counts regular, comparable, and equipped with a sampling theory.{{cite:dodge2008}} (pp. 152, 393)

Official series for employment, education, health, and political participation expanded as states built statistical offices and as international bodies, including the Statistical Commission, promoted comparable official statistics. The first world population census organised under those auspices was in 1950, and another followed in 1960. From the 1960s, indicator programmes in economics, health, and education tried to give politicians a system of information about the effects of policy. The choice of what to count remained partly political. The contents of official statistics expand or contract with the needs of political power, which is why the boundary of the subject has been hard to draw. The twentieth-century discipline added probability sampling and explicit tests for social systems. Kiaer's 1895 argument for representative samples, and the 1925 Rome congress that put random sampling beside purposive selection, are the sampling steps on which modern social surveys stand. International principles now put the social situation alongside the economic, the demographic, and the environmental as something official statistics are for. The General Assembly endorsed those principles on 29 January 2014, as Resolution 68/261, after the Statistical Commission had adopted them in 1994 and reaffirmed them in 2013.{{cite:dodge2008}} (pp. 394, 468–469, 519){{cite:un_res68261}} (Principle 1)
