## Lead context
Universities now award degrees in data science, and the courses overlap statistics without sitting inside statistics departments. Data science, in the sense argued for by people who work on the analysis of data, is the technical work of learning from data: models, computing, theory, and the evaluation of tools, judged by whether the analyst learns more.{{cite:cleveland2001}} (pp. 21–22){{cite:donoho2015}} (p. 1)

## Definition — Scope
William Cleveland used the name for an enlargement of statistics organised around the practising analyst. Six areas share the effort. Multidisciplinary investigations, the largest share in his plan, are collaborations on subject-matter data. Models and methods cover statistical models, model building, and probabilistic estimation. Computing with data covers hardware, software, and algorithms. Pedagogy, tool evaluation, and theory make up the rest. A tool counts if it helps the analyst directly; a theory counts if it helps build such tools. The Bayesian account of estimation is his example of a theory with that kind of reach.{{cite:cleveland2001}} (pp. 21–22)

David Donoho's later survey distinguishes a narrower public meaning from that programme. The public meaning is often a superset of statistics and machine learning plus the technology for scaling to large data, motivated by commercial demand. His alternative, which he calls greater data science, follows Tukey, Cleveland, Chambers, and Breiman: the science of learning from data. He groups the work into six divisions: exploration and preparation, representation and transformation, computing, visualisation, modelling, and science about data science itself.{{cite:donoho2015}} (pp. 1, 22–23)

## Statistical use
The model shelf is the statistical one. [Linear regression](multiple-linear-regression.qmd), [generalised linear models](generalised-linear-model.qmd), and [regularisation](regularisation.qmd) such as [ridge regression](ridge-regression.qmd) are standard when the number of predictors is large. [Cross-validation](cross-validation.qmd) and a [held-out test set](a-held-out-test-set.qmd) estimate how a fitted rule behaves on new cases. [Classification](classification-accuracy.qmd) and [regression trees](regression-tree.qmd), including [random forests](random-forest.qmd), are used when the goal is prediction. [Principal components](principal-component-directions.qmd) and other low-dimensional summaries handle [high-dimensional data](high-dimensional-data.qmd).

Exploration is not a preface to be skipped. Plots, transformations, and checks for artifacts are how an analyst sees whether the model class is even plausible. Tukey had already argued in 1962 that his central interest was data analysis, including procedures, interpretation, the planning of data collection, and the parts of mathematical statistics that serve those tasks. Computing is one of the six divisions: several languages, documented workflows, and packages that carry a step from one project to the next. Modelling splits, in Breiman's terms, into a generative culture aimed at the mechanism and a predictive culture aimed at accuracy on future inputs.{{cite:donoho2015}} (pp. 10, 22–24){{cite:cleveland2001}} (p. 22)

## History
John Tukey's 1962 paper "The Future of Data Analysis" is the usual intellectual start. He told mathematical statisticians that his central interest had become data analysis, and he treated that activity as a science rather than as a branch of mathematics. Later appeals pushed on different walls. John Chambers argued for more attention to preparing and presenting data. Bill Cleveland, in 2001, published the six-part plan and the name "data science," with computing and multidisciplinary projects given shares that a classical theory department did not have. Leo Breiman's 2001 essay described a generative culture, aimed at how inputs produce responses, and a predictive culture, aimed at future responses and largely silent about the mechanism. Around 2015, campus initiatives and new master's degrees made the name an administrative fact, often led from outside statistics departments, which is the tension Donoho's survey records.{{cite:cleveland2001}} (pp. 21–22){{cite:donoho2015}} (pp. 1, 10, section 5)

## Connections
Statistics remains the parent discipline for the probability models, the [sampling distribution](sampling-distribution.qmd), and the [design of experiments](designed-experiment.qmd). Machine learning is the predictive culture Breiman located largely outside statistics departments. [Econometrics](econometrics.qmd), [biostatistics](biostatistics.qmd), and [epidemiology](epidemiology.qmd) are subject-matter fields that already did multidisciplinary data analysis under other names. The argument for a separate name is organisational as much as mathematical: computing, data preparation, and the evaluation of tools have to be taught and staffed, or the modelling core has nothing reliable to model.{{cite:cleveland2001}} (p. 22){{cite:donoho2015}} (pp. 22–24)
