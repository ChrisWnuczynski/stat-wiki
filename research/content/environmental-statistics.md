## Lead context

A river's quality, a forest's area, and the waste a factory discharges are not one survey. Environmental statistics describes the state of the environment, the human and natural pressures on it, and the responses to those pressures. The subject is multi-disciplinary, its sources are dispersed across agencies, and the methods used to compile it are a mixture of censuses, surveys, administrative records, and instrument networks. The product is a synthesis used to formulate and evaluate policies that cut across the economy and the environment.{{cite:un_fdes1984}} (executive summary, para. 1, p. 1)

## Definition

There is no single classification of the environment from which a definition drops out. A survey of national practices found it easier to list the pieces than to give one formula, and the 1984 framework does not try to impose an a priori definition beyond statistics related to the environment. What it fixes is the scope that both developing and industrialised countries have used. That scope includes the media of the natural environment, air, land and soil, and water, together with the biota in those media, human settlements, natural disasters, pollution, and natural resources. Priorities differ by country. The list of pieces does not.{{cite:un_fdes1984}} (paras. 11–13, p. 4; executive summary, para. 1, p. 1)

The statistics inside that scope cover natural phenomena and human activities that affect the environment, the impacts of those activities and events on the environment and on human welfare, and the means of improving conditions. They cover qualitative and quantitative aspects. They mix conventional statistics, monitoring data, and remote-sensing information. The records themselves often come from somewhere else: economic data from enterprises and establishments, ecological data on geographical units, readings from monitoring stations, and social and demographic data from households and individuals. A number about emissions can begin life as a production return. A number about exposure can begin life as a health survey. Environment statistics is the synthesis that puts those series into one framework.{{cite:un_fdes1984}} (paras. 13–14, pp. 4–5)

The framework organises the pieces as components crossed with information categories. The components are flora, fauna, atmosphere, water (fresh and marine), land and soil (surface and sub-surface), and human settlements. The information categories follow a sequence of action, impact, and reaction, plus background: social and economic activities and natural events, the environmental impacts of those activities and events, the responses to the impacts, and inventories, stocks, and background conditions. The cells of that table are statistical topics, the quantifiable aspects of general environmental concerns. Identifying a topic is a step toward identifying a variable. It is not yet the variable, the classification, or the estimator.{{cite:un_fdes1984}} (executive summary, paras. 3–4)

## Statistical use

Collection is a mixture of [censuses](census.qmd), [surveys](stratified-and-cluster-sampling.qmd), administrative records, and monitoring from ground stations and from the air, including remote sensing. Producers and users include government departments, central statistical services, research institutes, local authorities, and international organisations, and further demand comes from business, scientists, the press, and the public. A central statistical office, or an interdepartmental committee, is the coordination the framework recommends when those producers would otherwise collect the same series twice and publish neither of them on time. International organisations ask for series that can be compared across a boundary, which is a stronger demand than a national compendium that only has to be coherent at home.{{cite:un_fdes1984}} (paras. 17–20, pp. 5–6)

A monitoring design that selects sites by a probability sample can support an estimate for a target population of lakes, streams, or forest stands, rather than a description of the sites that were convenient to visit. The U.S. Environmental Monitoring and Assessment Program made that link a requirement. A probability sample has three characteristics: the population is described explicitly, every element has a known chance of selection, and the draw includes an explicit random step. Design-based inference extends the sample to that population only when the sample is of this kind. The design says what is measured and where. A protocol says how the inference is drawn. Together they are the sampling plan. Most of the methods estimate a cumulative distribution or its variance under the survey design that was used. When sample units are missing, one response is to shrink the target population to the units that could be measured. If instead the original population is kept and the missing units are treated as missing at random, proportion estimators of the cumulative distribution stay unbiased, while estimators of totals and of distributions expressed as amounts, counts, lengths, or areas, are biased.{{cite:diaz_ramos1996}} (pp. 1–3, 10–11)

[Geostatistics](geostatistics.qmd) enters when the environmental quantity is a concentration with coordinates and the question is a map, not only a regional mean. A [confidence interval](confidence-interval.qmd) for a status estimate is the uncertainty that a policy comparison has to carry. Remote sensing and monitoring stations add data that are not a household survey and still have to be summarised with a stated coverage and a stated time. The framework's background category is where those inventories sit: stocks and conditions against which an impact is judged.{{cite:un_fdes1984}} (executive summary, para. 3){{cite:diaz_ramos1996}} (p. 3)

## Common misconceptions

The framework is not a list of indicators, and it is not a methods manual. It identifies statistical topics. It does not specify the parameters, the indicators, the classifications, the tabulations, or the methods of collection. Those were left to later guidelines for particular environmental areas, written so that the areas would not overlap. Treating the framework table as if each cell were already a published indicator confuses a classification of concerns with a measurement.{{cite:un_fdes1984}} (executive summary, para. 4)

A convenient set of monitoring sites is not an estimate for a region. Design-based inference from a sample to a population of lakes or stream segments holds for a probability sample of that population. A network placed where the road is close, or where a previous study found a problem, describes those sites. It does not, without a model that is doing different work, describe the lakes that were never eligible to be visited. Model-based spatial prediction is a separate argument and has to be stated as such. The cumulative-distribution estimator and the variance that goes with it are design quantities. They are not properties of the instrument alone.{{cite:diaz_ramos1996}} (p. 3)

A series compiled for another purpose does not become an environment statistic merely by being reprinted. Production, household, and health records enter the synthesis when the framework says which component and which information category they belong to, and when the coverage and the time are the ones the policy comparison uses. An economic total for a different year, or a health rate for a different population, is still that other series.{{cite:un_fdes1984}} (para. 14, p. 5)

## Connections

[Demography](demography.qmd) and economic statistics contribute many of the human and production series that environment statistics reuse. [Epidemiology](epidemiology.qmd) shares the health impacts of pollution and of disasters. [Geostatistics](geostatistics.qmd) shares the spatial prediction of a concentration. Official statistics is the institutional home of the compendiums, and the System of National Accounts is one of the neighbouring frameworks the environment tables have to line up with when a response is a tax or a subsidy. The statistical obligation is the same in each case. A number about the environment is an estimate for a stated population, time, and place, produced by a method that matches how the sites or the records were chosen.{{cite:un_fdes1984}} (paras. 13–17, pp. 4–5){{cite:diaz_ramos1996}} (pp. 2–3)

## History

International work on environment statistics took shape in the Conference of European Statisticians. A March 1973 meeting on statistics for environmental studies and policies called for guidelines and, at the start, for a minimum data base rather than a finished system. A Warsaw seminar in October 1973 put pollution data first and asked for a continuing exchange of national practice. The United Nations Conference on the Human Environment had already stated the global concern. In 1974 the Statistical Commission preferred a step-by-step approach, aimed at needs, availability, and methodological guidelines, over the construction of a complex system. A survey of country practices then showed that the field needed a structure. In 1979 the Commission asked the Statistical Office to explore a framework. A first draft, discussed in 1981, was used in regional workshops and pilot projects and was judged too complex for worldwide use. A revised draft was reviewed by experts in September 1982, and in 1983 the Commission requested publication. The 1984 framework is that publication. Its objective is to help develop, coordinate, and organise environment statistics at national and international level, on the basis of structures countries were already using, with the explicit expectation that national conditions would require adaptation.{{cite:un_fdes1984}} (paras. 5–9, p. 3)

In the United States, the Environmental Monitoring and Assessment Program was begun to monitor the status and trends of ecological resources and to build methods for emerging problems. Its statistical methods, written for the regional demonstration studies, put probability sampling and design-based estimators at the centre of a status assessment. The 1996 manual is the estimator side of that programme: cumulative distributions, inclusion probabilities, and the requirement that the analysis follow the design.{{cite:diaz_ramos1996}} (pp. 2–3)
