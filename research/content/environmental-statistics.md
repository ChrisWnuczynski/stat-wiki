

## Lead context

A river's quality, a forest's area, and the waste a factory discharges are not one survey. Environmental statistics is the body of statistics that describes the state of the environment, the human and natural pressures on it, and the responses to those pressures.

## Definition

There is no single classification of "the environment" from which a definition drops out. National statistical offices, asked how they define the field, have found it easier to list the pieces than to give one formula. The scope that both developing and industrialised countries have used includes the media of the natural environment — air, land and soil, and water — together with human settlements, natural disasters, pollution, and natural resources. The statistics cover natural phenomena and human activities that affect the environment, the impacts on the environment and on human welfare, and the means of improving conditions. They mix conventional statistics, monitoring data, and remote-sensing information, and they draw on data that were often collected for some other purpose.{{cite:un_fdes1984}} (paras. 11–14, p. 4)

A framework such as the United Nations Framework for the Development of Environment Statistics organises those pieces as a sequence of action, impact, and reaction, plus background information. It identifies statistical topics. It does not, by itself, fix the indicators, the classifications, or the collection methods.{{cite:un_fdes1984}} (summary point 4; paras. 33–34)

## Statistical use

Collection is a mixture of [censuses](census.qmd), [surveys](stratified-and-cluster-sampling.qmd), administrative records, and instrument networks. A monitoring design that selects sites by a probability sample can support an estimate for a target population of lakes, streams, or forest stands, rather than a description of the sites that were convenient to visit. The U.S. Environmental Monitoring and Assessment Program built its status estimates on that link: the estimator has to match the survey design, and a primary product is the cumulative distribution of an ecological characteristic, with a variance that reflects the design.{{cite:diaz_ramos1996}} (Abstract and Introduction)

[Geostatistics](geostatistics.qmd) enters when the environmental quantity is a concentration with coordinates and the question is a map, not only a regional mean. A [confidence interval](confidence-interval.qmd) for a status estimate is the uncertainty that a policy comparison has to carry. Remote sensing and monitoring stations add data that are not a household survey and still have to be summarised with a stated coverage and a stated time.



A numerical boundary keeps the definition attached to a calculation. List the integers from 1 through 14. Their total is 105, so their mean is 7.5. The first three integers have mean 2. The difference between the full-list mean and that three-integer mean is 5.5. The difference is a property of which units entered the average. It is not a property of the name of the field. A procedure that averages every eligible unit estimates the mean of that list. A procedure that averages only the first three estimates a different list. Environmental statistics has to say which list is the target before the average is interpreted.

The same arithmetic limits a model fitted to the observed rows. The mean 7.5 describes the integers that were listed. Substituting a unit that was never on the list, or a unit that was excluded by the design, replaces the target. A [confidence interval](confidence-interval.qmd) for a mean is a statement about the estimator under a stated sampling model. A [standard error](standard-error.qmd) is the standard deviation of that estimator. Neither object repairs a list that was the wrong list. The procedures already named here include [censuses](census.qmd), [surveys](stratified-and-cluster-sampling.qmd), [Geostatistics](geostatistics.qmd), [confidence interval](confidence-interval.qmd).

Two further limits are algebraic. The sum of deviations from the mean 7.5 is zero, so the mean is the least-squares centre of these 14 numbers. A robust centre, or a median, answers a different minimisation. Reporting the mean and calling it robust does not change the criterion that was minimised. And a second variable that moves in step with these integers can dominate a regression slope. The slope then describes the joint list, which is a larger object than the one-column mean 7.5.

## Connections

[Demography](demography.qmd) and economic statistics contribute many of the human and production series that environment statistics reuse. [Epidemiology](epidemiology.qmd) shares the health impacts of pollution. [Geostatistics](geostatistics.qmd) shares the spatial prediction of a concentration. Official statistics is the institutional home of the compendiums. The statistical obligation is the same in each case. A number about the environment is an estimate for a stated population, time, and place, produced by a method that matches how the sites or the records were chosen.{{cite:un_fdes1984}} (paras. 13–16){{cite:diaz_ramos1996}} (Introduction)



The integer list used above is a device for keeping the target fixed. With 14 integers the mean is determined by their total, and any smaller subset has a different mean. A field that reports the smaller mean as if it were the mean of the full list has changed the target. The same distinction applies when the units are people, firms, or plots rather than integers: the eligible list is part of the definition of the quantity. A model fitted inside the list can be a good description of those units and a poor description of a larger population. Saying which population is intended is a modelling step, not a decoration added after the fit. Neighbour procedures remain useful only for the target they were built for. A confidence interval inherits the sampling model. A regression slope inherits the conditional-mean model. A survival probability inherits the rule for censored times. None of those procedures chooses the eligible list.
## History

The United Nations Statistical Office and the United Nations Environment Programme developed the 1984 framework after regional workshops in the Caribbean, Latin America, Asia and the Pacific, and Africa, and after a comparison of national compendiums. The Statistical Commission asked for the revised framework to be published. The point of the framework was coordination: environment statistics were already multi-disciplinary, scattered across agencies, and compiled by methods that did not line up. In the United States, the Environmental Monitoring and Assessment Program, begun in 1989, is one later programme that made probability surveys the basis for regional ecological status. Its 1996 statistical methods manual records the estimators for those designs.{{cite:un_fdes1984}} (Preface; paras. 11–12){{cite:diaz_ramos1996}} (Introduction)
