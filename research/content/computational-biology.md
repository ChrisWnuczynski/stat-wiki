## Lead context
A genome is a string, an expression assay is a table, and a protein structure is a set of coordinates. Computational biology is the development and use of data-analytic, theoretical, and simulation methods on biological systems, as distinct from the tools that only store and display those data. The distinction was written down because the two activities were already being practised under overlapping names.{{cite:nih_bistic2000}} (17 July 2000, Preamble and Definition)

## Definition
The NIH Biomedical Information Science and Technology Initiative, in a definition released on 17 July 2000, separated two overlapping activities. Bioinformatics is the research, development, or application of computational tools and approaches for expanding the use of biological, medical, behavioural, or health data, including tools to acquire, store, organise, archive, analyse, or visualise such data. Computational biology is the development and application of data-analytical and theoretical methods, mathematical modelling, and computational simulation techniques to the study of biological, behavioural, and social systems. The consortium noted that no wording removes the overlap, and that different organisations will still draw the line in different places.{{cite:nih_bistic2000}} (17 July 2000, Preamble and Definition)

Both activities sit in the life sciences and in computing. The preamble places them in mathematics, physics, computer science, engineering, biology, and behavioural science, and it says that each keeps a close interaction with the life sciences if it is to do the work it is named for. Bioinformatics, in that wording, applies principles of information science so that large and heterogeneous life-science data become understandable and useful. Computational biology uses mathematical and computational approaches on theoretical and experimental questions in biology. The data-analytic half of the second definition is ordinary statistics aimed at a biological object. The theoretical half is the design of the method: an algorithm, a model, or a likelihood for a history that was not observed.{{cite:nih_bistic2000}} (17 July 2000, Preamble and Definition)

[Biostatistics](biostatistics.qmd) is the neighbour that grew up around human biology and medicine. Dodge's encyclopedia entry places its domains in epidemiology, clinical trials, biological trials, and the ethical questions those trials raise, and it lists the methods in routine use there: resampling, multivariate analysis, regression, the evaluation of uncertainty in an estimate, and the treatment of missing data. Computational biology is the neighbour that grew up around molecular and systems questions and the models those data can identify. A methods paper can belong to both, which is the overlap the 2000 wording refused to legislate away.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 49) {{cite:nih_bistic2000}} (Definition)

## Statistical use
The data-analytic half of the NIH definition is the part a statistician recognises. [Maximum likelihood](maximum-likelihood.qmd) fits a sequence model or a dose–response when the observations are treated as draws from a stated distribution. [Least squares](least-squares.qmd) and other regression methods relate an expression, a concentration, or a count to the conditions that produced it. [Clustering](hierarchical-clustering.qmd) and [principal components](principal-component-directions.qmd) organise high-dimensional assays when the scientific question is a grouping or a low-dimensional summary rather than a single coefficient. Simulation is the tool the definition names for the case in which a stochastic model of a pathway or a population is easier to run than to solve in closed form.{{cite:nih_bistic2000}} (Definition) {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 49)

The same list appears, under another name, in the biostatistics entry. Resampling methods such as the bootstrap and the jackknife are used when a standard error is wanted and the sampling distribution is awkward. Multivariate analysis is the natural language for an assay that records thousands of features on each sample. Regression, including the forms that handle a binary or a counted response, is how a treatment, a genotype, or a dose is tied to an outcome. Missing data are ordinary: an assay fails, a time point is skipped, a sequence read does not cover a base. The statistical content of the field is the model and the inference. The computational content is the scale at which that inference has to run, and the algorithms that make the model identifiable.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 49)

A comparative study in this setting still has a design. Samples are units. Features are variables. A contrast between conditions is an effect only when the assignment, or an argument that replaces assignment, makes the groups comparable. Calling the work computational does not replace that requirement. It changes the size of the table and the form of the likelihood. Sequence comparison, phylogenetic models, and gene-finding rules are molecular tools that sit on the bioinformatics side of the 2000 wording when the emphasis is the software, and on the computational-biology side when the emphasis is the probabilistic model those tools implement. The consortium's point is that the hand-off is not clean.{{cite:nih_bistic2000}} (Preamble and Definition)

Uncertainty belongs in the report. An alignment score, a cluster label, or a predicted structure is an estimate. Dodge's list of biostatistical methods puts the evaluation of uncertainty next to the estimate itself. A point prediction without a statement of how it would move under a different sample, or under a different model, is an incomplete use of the data-analytic half of the definition.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 49)

## Common misconceptions
The two names are not a partition of papers into a software pile and a theory pile. The 2000 wording says the activities are distinct and also overlap, and it says explicitly that no definition removes that overlap or stops a laboratory from using one word for work another laboratory would file under the other. Treating the labels as a clean taxonomy misreads the document that introduced them as working definitions.{{cite:nih_bistic2000}} (Preamble and Definition)

Computational biology is not biostatistics under a newer name, and it is not a refusal of biostatistics. Biostatistics, in the encyclopedia account, is statistics applied to questions in human biology and medicine, with epidemiology and trials as principal domains. Computational biology, in the NIH wording, is data-analytic and theoretical method aimed at biological, behavioural, and social systems, with simulation included. A clinical trial analysed by a regression is biostatistics. A stochastic model of a molecular pathway, fitted by likelihood and checked by simulation, is the second definition. The same regression can appear in both.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 49) {{cite:nih_bistic2000}} (Definition)

Storing a sequence, drawing it, or serving it from a database is the bioinformatics half of the wording: acquire, store, organise, archive, analyse, or visualise. That work is necessary and it is not, by itself, the development of a data-analytic method. The definition separates the tool that makes the data usable from the method that studies the system. A pipeline that only reformats files has not yet done the second job. A likelihood that cannot be computed on the files has not done the first.{{cite:nih_bistic2000}} (Definition)

## Connections
[Biostatistics](biostatistics.qmd) shares likelihood, regression, resampling, and the design of comparative studies. [Epidemiology](epidemiology.qmd) shares the population questions when the biological system is a group of hosts rather than a molecule. The history Dodge gives for biostatistics starts from the London bills of mortality and from Graunt and Petty, which is also the usual start of [demography](demography.qmd), and it continues through Mendel's counts, Galton and Pearson on resemblance between parents and children, and Fisher's randomisation at Rothamsted. Those are the statistical ancestors of both fields.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 49)

[Cluster analysis](cluster-analysis.qmd) and principal-component summaries are the unsupervised tools a high-dimensional assay invites. [Maximum likelihood](maximum-likelihood.qmd) is the fitting principle when a probabilistic model of the observation is written down. The NIH definition's phrase "mathematical modelling and computational simulation" is the warrant for using those tools on a biological system, and for building a new one when the existing tools do not match the sampling process.{{cite:nih_bistic2000}} (Definition) {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 49)

## History
The working definitions were released on 17 July 2000 by the BISTIC Definition Committee, chaired by Michael Huerta, because the phrases were already in use for overlapping work and a consortium needed a wording it could share. The preamble roots both activities in the life sciences and in computing, and it refuses a wording that would pretend the overlap is empty. The definition that follows is short on purpose: bioinformatics is the computational tooling of biological, medical, behavioural, or health data, and computational biology is the data-analytic, theoretical, and simulation study of biological, behavioural, and social systems.{{cite:nih_bistic2000}} (17 July 2000, Preamble and Definition)

The statistical neighbour has a longer history under another name. Biostatistics, as Dodge records it, begins in the middle of the seventeenth century with Petty and Graunt on the London bills of mortality, applied to death rates, birth rates, and census studies. Mendel's plant counts in the nineteenth century, the founding of *Biometrika* by Pearson, Galton, and Weldon, and Fisher's random allocation of treatments are later steps in the same line. The methods Dodge lists as now common in that field — bootstrap and jackknife, multivariate analysis, regression, uncertainty of estimates, missing data — are the methods a computational-biology analysis uses when the object is a table of biological measurements rather than a clinical endpoint alone. The 2000 wording did not replace that history. It named the molecular and systems work that had grown up beside it, and it left the boundary porous.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 49) {{cite:nih_bistic2000}} (Definition)
