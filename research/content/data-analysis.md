## Lead context
A study of 451 patients at risk of stroke records a group, a 30-day outcome, and a 365-day outcome on every row. Reading those rows one at a time does not answer whether stents reduced strokes. The summary that puts the counts in one table, treatment beside control, is already a data analysis. In the stent trial, 33 of the treated patients and 13 of the control patients had a stroke within 30 days, and 45 of 224 treated patients and 28 of 227 control patients had a stroke by the end of the first year.{{cite:openintro2019}} (Ch. 1, §1.1)

## Definition
Data analysis is the work of getting information out of observations once they have been collected and arranged, usually in a table. The data are quantitative or qualitative, discrete or continuous, according to the variable they record. In a wide sense the activity is the part of statistics to which the rest of the subject is attached.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 150)

A first course describes the same activity as the step that follows collection: the rows are examined together, summary numbers are computed, and a conclusion is drawn about the question the study was built to ask. The stent table is the pattern. Considering each patient in turn is a long path. A statistical analysis considers the data at once, and a summary statistic is a single number that compresses a large table. The two proportions $45/224 = 0.20$ and $28/227 = 0.12$ are that kind of compression.{{cite:openintro2019}} (Ch. 1, §1.1)

The activity splits into recognisable kinds of work. Exploratory analysis graphs the data, transforms them when the scale is awkward, looks for outliers, looks for hypotheses that were not stated in advance, and considers robust summaries. Initial analysis chooses which methods to apply. Multivariate analysis reduces dimension and searches for structure, as in discriminant analysis. Correspondence analysis and classification are tools aimed at particular kinds of table. Confirmatory analysis estimates parameters, tests hypotheses, and states what the results support.{{cite:dodge2008}} (pp. 150–151)

Exploratory analysis is a stance as much as a list of techniques. The NIST handbook describes it as an approach that uses mostly graphical technique to maximise insight, uncover structure, extract important variables, detect outliers and anomalies, test assumptions, develop simpler models, and settle factor settings. It postpones the assumption that a named model is already the right one, and it lets the data show structure. It uses statistical graphics constantly, and it is a larger activity than the graphics alone.{{cite:nist_eda2002}} (§1.1.1, “Approach”, “Philosophy”)

## Properties
Start with the structure of the table: which variables were measured, which are numerical, and which pairs might move together. A [scatterplot](scatterplot.qmd) provides a case-by-case view of two numerical variables, one point for each case.{{cite:openintro2019}} (Ch. 2, §2.1.1) A [histogram](histogram.qmd) bins a single numerical variable and shows where the values sit, how they spread, and whether the shape is skewed.{{cite:openintro2019}} (Ch. 2, §2.1.3) A [line chart](line-chart.qmd) is a frequency graph for a discrete variable: the values sit on the horizontal axis and the frequencies on the vertical, with a line over each value.{{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 316) [Summary statistics](summary-statistics.qmd) compress centre, spread, and shape into a few numbers after those pictures have been read. The stent proportions are summary statistics in that sense.{{cite:openintro2019}} (Ch. 1, §1.1)

The exploratory aims and the confirmatory aims answer different questions about the same table. The seven aims in the handbook — insight, structure, important variables, anomalies, assumptions, simpler models, factor settings — sit before a named test. The confirmatory layer estimates a parameter, tests a hypothesis, and states a conclusion that is meant to travel beyond the rows in hand. Dodge lists those three confirmatory tasks explicitly, and lists the exploratory tasks as a separate class. Mixing them without saying which one is under way is how a pattern found by looking becomes a claim that was supposedly fixed in advance.{{cite:dodge2008}} (pp. 150–151) {{cite:nist_eda2002}} (§1.1.1, “Approach”)

Robust summaries belong with the exploratory class. A mean is pulled by a few extreme rows. A display that shows those rows, and a summary that resists them, are part of the first pass Dodge describes. Transformation belongs there too, when the scale makes a relationship hard to see. The choice of method, which Dodge calls initial data analysis, comes after that pass and before the confirmatory arithmetic. Multivariate reduction — fewer dimensions, a search for structure, discriminant analysis — is a further class, used when the table is wide. Correspondence analysis and classification are named as tools for particular tables rather than as the whole of the subject.{{cite:dodge2008}} (pp. 150–151)

The stent comparison shows what the confirmatory end looks like when the design has already fixed the question. The rows have been counted. The comparison of treatment with control is the quantity the study was built to produce. The surprise in that study, more strokes in the stent arm, is a property of the summary, and it is the reason the analysis exists: the table can contradict the expectation that motivated the trial. A summary that only restates the hope is not an analysis.{{cite:openintro2019}} (Ch. 1, §1.1)

Graphics and numerical reduction are complementary, not substitutes. The handbook's philosophy is that exploratory analysis is not identical to statistical graphics, even though the two phrases are often used as if they were. Graphics are a collection of techniques, each aimed at one aspect of the data. The analysis is the decision about what to look for, how to look, and how to interpret what appears. A [histogram](histogram.qmd), a [scatterplot](scatterplot.qmd), and a [line chart](line-chart.qmd) answer different questions. Centre, spread, and shape, once the picture has been read, are the job of [summary statistics](summary-statistics.qmd).{{cite:nist_eda2002}} (§1.1.1, “Philosophy”) {{cite:openintro2019}} (Ch. 2, §2.1.1; §2.1.3) {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 316)

## History
The exploratory stance was set out by Tukey. Dodge dates the first publication to a limited preliminary edition in 1970–1971, and *Exploratory Data Analysis* to 1977. The handbook names that 1977 book as the seminal work, and it notes later contributions, including Mosteller and Tukey on data analysis and regression and Hoaglin on interactive data analysis. Correspondence analysis has an earlier theoretical line, in Hirschfeld (1935), writing under that name, and Fisher (1940), with a later development by Benzécri in the 1970s. Numerical classification grew out of biological typology. Galen is cited for an ancient typology. Modern clustering methods are traced to Adanson in the eighteenth century and to Zubin (1938) and Thorndike (1953). Confirmatory estimation and testing are the older inferential layer on which those exploratory tools were laid. Dodge's point is that a single history of "data analysis" is hard to write because the name covers all of these strands.{{cite:dodge2008}} (pp. 150–151) {{cite:nist_eda2002}} (§1.1.1, “History”)

## Connections
[Statistical graphics](statistical-graphics.qmd) are the displays exploratory analysis relies on, and they are not the whole of the analysis. A [histogram](histogram.qmd), a [scatterplot](scatterplot.qmd), and a [line chart](line-chart.qmd) answer different questions about the same table. [Summary statistics](summary-statistics.qmd) are the numerical reduction that follows. Spread, shape, and tails continue in [statistical dispersion](statistical-dispersion.qmd), [skewness](skewness.qmd), and [kurtosis](kurtosis.qmd). A forecast comparison such as the [mean absolute percentage error](mean-absolute-percentage-error.qmd) is a later, confirmatory summary of errors rather than a first look at the series.{{cite:nist_eda2002}} (§1.1.1, “Philosophy”) {{cite:dodge2008}} (pp. 150–151, 316)

Classification, in the wide sense Dodge uses inside this entry, is one of the specific tools, not a synonym for the whole analysis. Cluster methods and correspondence analysis sit in the same list. The confirmatory triple — estimation, testing, and the conclusion that generalises — is what links data analysis to the rest of statistical inference. The exploratory pass is what keeps that triple from being applied to a table nobody has looked at.{{cite:dodge2008}} (pp. 150–151)
