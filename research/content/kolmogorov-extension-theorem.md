## Lead context
The theorem turns mutually compatible finite descriptions into one probability model for an entire sequence.

## Motivating example
A sequence of daily pass/fail tests can be specified by assigning a probability to each finite run. The assignments must agree when the last day is summed out. Otherwise the probability of passing on Monday would depend on whether Tuesday was also observed. Consistency is the condition that lets all these finite experiments belong to one infinite sequence.{{cite:durrett2019}} (§A.3, Theorem A.3.1, pp. 464–465)

## Definition — Consistency of finite windows

[]{#property-general-extension}
For each positive integer $n$, let $\mu_n$ be a probability law on $\mathbb R^n$. Suppose its marginal on the first $n-1$ coordinates is $\mu_{n-1}$. Then there is a unique probability law $P$ on $\mathbb R^{\mathbb N}$, equipped with the product sigma algebra, whose first $n$ coordinates have law $\mu_n$ for every $n$. The coordinate maps $X_n(\omega)=\omega_n$ form the desired stochastic process.{{cite:durrett2019}} (§A.3, Theorem A.3.1, p. 464)

For a general index set, finite laws must also respect permutations of coordinates and deletion of any coordinate. Real state spaces are an important case where these consistency requirements suffice: their finite-dimensional Borel probability laws are inner regular. The sigma algebra is generated by cylinder events, which constrain finitely many coordinates. Uniqueness refers to that sigma algebra; it does not assert that every subset of the path space is measurable.{{cite:tao2011_measure}} (§2.4, Thm. 2.4.3, pp. 237–238){{cite:durrett2019}} (§7.1, Thm. 7.1.1 and the discussion of the path sigma algebra, pp. 356–357)

## Properties — Existence and path regularity
A model for a sequence and a model with continuous sample paths answer different questions. Gaussian finite laws with covariance $\min(s,t)$ specify Brownian finite-dimensional distributions. Constructing the process on continuous functions requires an additional regularity argument. For an uncountable time set, the event of continuity is not automatically an event of the raw product sigma algebra.{{cite:durrett2019}} (§7.1, discussion following Thm. 7.1.1, p. 356)

For discrete time, the construction can be seen directly in a Markov model: an initial distribution $\pi$ and a transition matrix $K$ assign a path $(x_0,\ldots,x_n)$ probability $\pi(x_0)\prod_{j=1}^nK(x_{j-1},x_j)$. Summing over $x_n$ removes the last factor because a row of $K$ sums to one. Thus the path laws are consistent.{{cite:durrett2019}} (§5.1, Markov-chain finite-dimensional distributions)

## Worked example
Let $X_1,X_2,X_3$ be independent Bernoulli variables with success probability $0.3$. The finite law assigns the path $(1,0,1)$ probability $0.3^2(0.7)=0.063$ and $(1,0,0)$ probability $0.3(0.7)^2=0.147$. Their sum is $0.21$, which is exactly the assigned probability of $(X_1,X_2)=(1,0)$. The same summation works for every prefix length. The extension theorem guarantees one infinite Bernoulli sequence having these finite laws.{{cite:durrett2019}} (§A.3, Theorem A.3.1)

**Your turn.** In this model, sum the probabilities of $(1,1,0)$ and $(1,1,1)$. Which two-coordinate event results?

::: {.callout-tip collapse="true" title="Answer"}
The probabilities are $0.063$ and $0.027$. Their sum is $0.09=P(X_1=1,X_2=1)$.
:::
