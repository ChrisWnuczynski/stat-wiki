## Lead context
A discrete variable is often summarised by how often each value occurs. A line chart draws that count as the length of a line standing on the value. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 316) {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Frequency”)

## Motivating example
A die is thrown 20 times and the face 6 appears 4 times, the face 1 appears 2 times, and the other faces fill out the rest. A line chart puts the faces 1 through 6 along the horizontal axis and draws a vertical line at 6 whose length is 4. The line is a frequency, not a probability, until a model is added. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 316) {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 208)

## Intuition
The chart is a frequency table drawn instead of written. Each possible value gets one line. Taller lines are more common values. Because the variable is discrete, the lines stand on separated points rather than filling a bar of width. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 316) {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Frequency distribution”)

## Definition
A line chart is a frequency graph for a discrete variable. The values lie on the horizontal axis. Over each value $x_i$ a line is drawn whose length is the absolute frequency $n_i$, or the relative frequency $f_i$. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 316) A frequency is the number of times a value has been observed, and a relative frequency is that count divided by the number of observations. {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Absolute frequency”) {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Relative frequency”)

A frequency distribution is a display that organises those counts so they can be read. {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Frequency distribution”)

## Properties
If the lines show absolute frequencies, their lengths sum to the number of observations. If they show relative frequencies, the lengths sum to 1. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 208) {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 316)

The chart does not connect the tips into a curve that pretends the variable is continuous. The lines stay on the discrete values. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 316) {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Discrete variable”)

## Statistical use
The chart is a descriptive display. It answers which values occurred and how often. It does not, by itself, estimate a probability or test a hypothesis. A probability mass function is a model for those relative frequencies, not a second copy of the chart. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 316) {{cite:nist_glossary2002}} (Glossary, “model”)

## Worked example
Five households have sizes 2, 2, 3, 4, 2. The value 2 has absolute frequency 3, the value 3 has frequency 1, and the value 4 has frequency 1. A line chart has lines of lengths 3, 1, and 1 at 2, 3, and 4. The relative-frequency chart uses $3/5$, $1/5$, and $1/5$. Those three relative frequencies sum to 1. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 316) {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Relative frequency”)

**Your turn.** One more household of size 3 is added. What are the absolute frequencies of 2 and of 3, and what is the new relative frequency of 2?

::: {.callout-tip collapse="true" title="Answer"}
There are now six households. The value 2 still appears three times, and 3 appears twice. The relative frequency of 2 is 3/6 = 1/2.
:::

## Common misconceptions
A line chart of frequencies is not a line drawn through a time series. The horizontal axis is the value of a discrete variable, not a date. Joining the tips and reading the slope between two values invents values the variable does not take. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 316) {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Discrete variable”)

A bar chart uses a column for each category, or for each ungrouped discrete value. A line chart instead draws a line whose length is the frequency, standing on the value itself. {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Bar chart”) {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 316)

## Assumptions and limits
The chart needs a discrete variable with a list of values. A continuous measurement has to be grouped before a frequency line makes sense, and the grouping is then part of the display. Empty values, if they are possible and did not occur, are lines of length zero, not omitted categories that the reader cannot see. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 316) {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Continuous variable”)

## Connections

## History
Drawing a frequency as the length of a line is one of the older ways to show a discrete count without a table. The chart remains a description of the observations that were kept. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 316) {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 518)
