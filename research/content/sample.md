## Lead context
Most populations are too large, or too costly, to observe in full. A sample is the part that is observed, and the reason for observing it is to say something about the rest. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 64) {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Sample (partial enumeration)”)

## Motivating example
A city wants the proportion of households with a working smoke alarm. Observing a designed subset, and then reporting a proportion for the city, uses a sample. The report is about the city only because the subset was chosen in order to stand in for the rest. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 468) {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 64)

## Intuition
The sample is a subset chosen so that studying it is a substitute for studying every unit. The substitute is faithful when every unit had a known chance to be included. It is misleading when the list itself excludes a kind of unit, as a telephone list once excluded households without telephones. {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Random (probability) sample”) {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 469) {{cite:nist_glossary2002}} (Glossary, “sample”)

## Definition
A sample is a subset of a population, the portion that is observed when the whole population is not. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 64) {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Sample (partial enumeration)”) Studies are made on the sample in order to draw conclusions about the population. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 468) In one sense the sample is the set of observational units. In another it is the collection of measurements actually observed on those units. {{cite:nist_glossary2002}} (Glossary, “sample”)

Sampling selects that subset, studies it, and infers from it back to the population. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 64) {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 468)

## Properties
The sample size is the number of units in the sample. {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Sample size”) A statistic is a number computed from the sample. A parameter is the corresponding feature of the population. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 412) {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Statistic”) {{cite:nist_glossary2002}} (Glossary, “statistic”)

A probability sample gives every unit a chance of selection that can be stated. A non-probability sample does not. {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Random (probability) sample”) {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Non-random (non-probability) sample”)

## Statistical use
Estimation and testing are operations on a sample that aim at a population. The sampling error is the discrepancy that exists only because the sample is not the census. {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Estimate”) {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Sampling error”) {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Inferential statistics”)

## Worked example
The population values are 2, 4, 6, 8, 10. Their mean is 6. The sample 2, 6, 10 has mean 6. The sample 2, 4, 6 has mean 4. The parameter did not change between the two samples. The statistic did. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 412) {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Sample (partial enumeration)”)

**Your turn.** The sample 2, 4, 6 is enlarged by the unit whose value is 8. What is the new sample mean, and what is the population mean?

::: {.callout-tip collapse="true" title="Answer"}
The sample values are 2, 4, 6, and 8. Their mean is 5. The population mean remains 6.
:::

## Common misconceptions
A sample is not a small census of a different population. If the frame excludes a class of units, the sample represents the frame, not the population that was named in the question. {{cite:nist_glossary2002}} (Glossary, “sample frame”) {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 469)

The sample mean is not "the mean", full stop. It is the mean of the units that were observed. {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Mean”) {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 412)

## Assumptions and limits
Random selection removes some kinds of favouritism. It does not remove measurement error, and it does not repair a frame that never listed the units of interest. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 191) {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Coverage”) {{cite:nist_glossary2002}} (Glossary, “sample”)

## History
Extrapolation from a part to a whole is old. A defensible account of when the part is representative is much newer, and it includes famous failures of lists that looked large and were not representative. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 468) {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 469)
