## Lead context
Some questions are about a population small enough, or important enough, that every unit is observed. That complete observation is a census. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 64) {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Census (complete enumeration)”)

## Motivating example
A school wants the number of pupils enrolled this morning. Asking every class and counting every name is a census of the enrolled pupils. Asking three classes and multiplying is a sample, even if the arithmetic is careful. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 64) {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Census (complete enumeration)”)

## Intuition
A census trades cost for coverage. Nothing is estimated about a unit that was skipped, because no unit is skipped. The price is that every unit must be reached. For a city or a country that price is large, which is why samples replaced censuses for many questions. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 64) {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 468)

## Definition
A census is an operation that observes all of the individuals in a population. The word covers a population count and any exhaustive inquiry that retrieves information by observing every unit. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 64) It is a study of every unit in the population, the method that assesses all the units in the frame. {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Census (complete enumeration)”) {{cite:nist_glossary2002}} (Glossary, “census”)

Sampling, by contrast, observes only a portion of the population. That portion is the sample. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 64) {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Sample (partial enumeration)”)

## Properties
A census of a finite population can still be wrong. People are missed, forms are duplicated, and answers are mistaken. Those are not sampling errors. Sampling error is the extra gap that appears only because a subset was used instead of the census. {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Sampling error”) {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Non-sampling error”) {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 64)

A census mean of a recorded variable, if every unit is measured without error, is the population mean. No interval is required for sampling variability, because there is none. Measurement error remains. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 412) {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 191)

## Statistical use
Official population counts, a roll of every student in a course, and a complete inventory of a warehouse are censuses. A survey may be a census or a sample: the word survey names the collection, not the coverage. {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Survey”) {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 64) When the population is large, the census is often judged too expensive and a sample is designed instead. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 64) {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 468)

## Worked example
Eight workshops form the population, with outputs 5, 7, 6, 8, 5, 9, 6, and 4 tonnes. A census total is 5+7+6+8+5+9+6+4 = 50, and the census mean is 50/8 = 6.25. A sample of three workshops, the first, the fourth, and the last, has mean (5+8+4)/3 = 17/3. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 64) {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Mean”)

**Your turn.** Two workshops are added, with outputs 7 and 9. What is the census mean of the ten? If the original sample of three is not enlarged, what is the sample mean?

::: {.callout-tip collapse="true" title="Answer"}
The census total is 50 + 7 + 9 = 66, so the census mean is 6.6. The sample mean stays 17/3.
:::

## Common misconceptions
A census is not a sample of everyone who was easy to reach. Missing the units that are hard to find turns the census into an incomplete list. A large sample is not a census, however small its sampling error. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 64) {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Coverage”) {{cite:nist_glossary2002}} (Glossary, “census”)

## Assumptions and limits
Exhaustive observation does not correct a badly defined unit or a badly worded question. The census and the sample can share the same measurement error. What the census removes is the error that comes only from observing a part. {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Non-sampling error”) {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 191)

## Connections
The [population](population.qmd) is what the census tries to cover. The [sample](sample.qmd) is the alternative, and a [simple random sample](simple-random-sample.qmd) is one way to choose it. A collection of information may cover every unit or only a subset. {{cite:abs_glossary2023}} (Glossary, “Survey”) {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 468)

## History
Counts of people and goods were made in the early states, for taxation and for armies, long before sampling was a respectable substitute. In Europe the seventeenth century already distinguished a descriptive tradition, a census tradition, and the English work that led toward modern statistics. {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 64) {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 65) {{cite:dodge2008}} (p. 518)
