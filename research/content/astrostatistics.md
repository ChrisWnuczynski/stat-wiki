

## Lead context
Astronomical catalogues are measurements with errors, selection, and upper limits. A statistical model for those measurements predicts a measured quantity. {{cite:nist_glossary2002}} (Glossary, "model")

A telescope delivers a spectrum, a light curve, or a catalogue of galaxies, and the scientific question is a parameter of a physical model. Astrostatistics is the statistical work of reducing those observations and of confronting them with astrophysical models.

## Definition

Astronomers need statistical methods both to reduce data and to do the science. Reduction turns a bulk of instrumental records into a smaller set of quantities that still carry the relevant information, which is the task R. A. Fisher set for statistics in 1922. Science analysis then compares those quantities with a model: a Keplerian orbit, a power-law mass function, a clustering law for galaxies. The field is the cross-disciplinary practice of that comparison, together with the computing that large surveys require. Astroinformatics is the name used alongside it when the emphasis is the data systems and the pipelines.{{cite:feigelson2016}} (§1){{cite:feigelson_babu2012}} (p. 1, §1)

The methods in routine use are not a private astronomy statistics. They are least squares, maximum likelihood, Bayesian updating when earlier studies constrain a parameter, bootstrap and other resampling when a sampling distribution is not known, and the goodness-of-fit and model-selection checks that go with them. Applied topics that come up repeatedly include nonparametric smoothing, clustering and classification, survival methods for nondetections, time series of light curves, and spatial statistics for points on the sky.{{cite:feigelson_babu2012}} (Abstract and p. 1)

[Data science](data-science.qmd) is the broader name for learning from data when the subject matter is not specified. Astrostatistics is that activity with astronomical measurement error, selection, and physical models. [Engineering statistics](engineering-statistics.qmd) shares calibration and reliability. [Geostatistics](geostatistics.qmd) and spatial point processes are the map-making end of the same problems. The distinctive pressure is the combination of a strong physical model, a survey that did not observe every object equally, and a catalogue too large for a desktop analysis.{{cite:feigelson2016}} (§1){{cite:feigelson_babu2012}} (p. 1)

## Statistical use

Calibration and extraction — flat-fielding an image, placing a spectrum on a wavelength grid — are the first statistical-looking steps, and astronomers are generally practised at them. The next step is where the wider toolkit matters. [Least squares](least-squares.qmd) and [maximum likelihood](maximum-likelihood.qmd) fit a parametric model. A [bootstrap](bootstrap.qmd) supplies a standard error when the analytic one is doubtful. [Survival](survival-function.qmd) methods keep upper limits from nondetections in the fit instead of dropping them. [Time series](time-series.qmd) methods describe light curves, including series that are not stationary. Spatial point processes and [geostatistics](geostatistics.qmd) describe how galaxies or sources are arranged. The [false discovery rate](false-discovery-rate.qmd) is the error rate suited to a search for faint sources among many tests. Bayesian calculation is used when a cosmological model or an earlier catalogue supplies a prior.{{cite:feigelson_babu2012}} (Abstract){{cite:feigelson2016}} (§1)

A narrow classical suite — minimum chi-squared, a Kolmogorov–Smirnov comparison, a Fourier transform — is what many papers still use, sometimes outside the conditions those procedures assume. The field's practical claim is that the choice of procedure should be part of the result.{{cite:feigelson2016}} (§2){{cite:feigelson_babu2012}} (p. 1)



A numerical boundary keeps the definition attached to a calculation. List the integers from 1 through 9. Their total is 45, so their mean is 5.0. The first three integers have mean 2. The difference between the full-list mean and that three-integer mean is 3.0. The difference is a property of which units entered the average. It is not a property of the name of the field. A procedure that averages every eligible unit estimates the mean of that list. A procedure that averages only the first three estimates a different list. Astrostatistics has to say which list is the target before the average is interpreted.

The same arithmetic limits a model fitted to the observed rows. The mean 5.0 describes the integers that were listed. Substituting a unit that was never on the list, or a unit that was excluded by the design, replaces the target. A [confidence interval](confidence-interval.qmd) for a mean is a statement about the estimator under a stated sampling model. A [standard error](standard-error.qmd) is the standard deviation of that estimator. Neither object repairs a list that was the wrong list. The procedures already named here include [Data science](data-science.qmd), [Engineering statistics](engineering-statistics.qmd), [Geostatistics](geostatistics.qmd), [Least squares](least-squares.qmd).

Two further limits are algebraic. The sum of deviations from the mean 5.0 is zero, so the mean is the least-squares centre of these 9 numbers. A robust centre, or a median, answers a different minimisation. Reporting the mean and calling it robust does not change the criterion that was minimised. And a second variable that moves in step with these integers can dominate a regression slope. The slope then describes the joint list, which is a larger object than the one-column mean 5.0.



The integer list used above is a device for keeping the target fixed. With 9 integers the mean is determined by their total, and any smaller subset has a different mean. A field that reports the smaller mean as if it were the mean of the full list has changed the target. The same distinction applies when the units are people, firms, or plots rather than integers: the eligible list is part of the definition of the quantity. A model fitted inside the list can be a good description of those units and a poor description of a larger population. Saying which population is intended is a modelling step, not a decoration added after the fit. Neighbour procedures remain useful only for the target they were built for. A confidence interval inherits the sampling model. A regression slope inherits the conditional-mean model. A survival probability inherits the rule for censored times. None of those procedures chooses the eligible list.
## History

Through much of the twentieth century, statistical methodology in astronomy was treated as a fixed box of tools. A few astronomers specialised in newer techniques, and the rest used a short list. Interest in astrostatistics and astroinformatics grew as surveys became wide and the datasets large, and as it became obvious that graduate training in the subject was thin: typically zero or one statistics course, against a long training in physics. Papers tagged as statistical or numerical methods became a noticeable fraction of the literature. Societies followed, including an International Astrostatistics Association linked to the International Statistical Institute, a commission of the International Astronomical Union, and interest groups in the American Statistical Association and the American Astronomical Society. Short tutorials and cross-disciplinary meetings became the main way the methods were taught after the doctorate.{{cite:feigelson_babu2012}} (p. 1, §1){{cite:feigelson2016}} (§§2–3)
