# Borel–Cantelli lemmas

Rank 339. Lighter tier. Theorem page.

## Facts checked

- Durrett, *Probability: Theory and Examples*, version 5 of 11 January 2019 (the fifth edition whose Cambridge printing is the bibliography record). Theorem 2.3.1, p. 68: summable probabilities imply \(\mathrm{P}(A_n\text{ i.o.})=0\), proved by finiteness of \(\mathrm{E}(N)\). Theorem 2.3.7, p. 70: independent events and a divergent series imply probability \(1\), by the bound \(1-x\le e^{-x}\). The same page says the converse of the first lemma is false; the printed counterexample is a nested family of intervals, not the repeated-event example. The repeated event of probability \(1/2\) is checked directly: the infinitely-often event equals that single event.
- Geometric tail \(\sum_{n\ge 5}2^{-n}=1/16\) and partial sum \(15/16\). Harmonic series diverges. Repeated event of probability \(1/2\) keeps probability \(1/2\).

- Printed page numbers in the author PDF match the fifth-edition pages used for the citation. The Cambridge imprint was not opened as a separate file.

## Disputed

- None. The page's union-bound write-up and Durrett's expectation write-up are the same first lemma.

## Not opened
