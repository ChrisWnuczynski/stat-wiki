# Extreme-value classification

Rank 361. Type: concept. Tier: lighter.

## Checked
- NIST/SEMATECH §8.1.6.3: for a well-behaved initial distribution only a few limit models are needed, depending on the maximum or the minimum and on whether the variable is bounded; the Type I (Gumbel) law is the limit for an unbounded minimum, and a lower bound leads to the Weibull.
- The exponential, Pareto and uniform finite-n formulas, and the numbers 0.35849, 0.36788, 0.34868, 0.36417, 0.00939 and 0.00371, were recomputed from (1−a/n)^n and e^{−1}.
- Fisher–Tippett (1928), Gnedenko (1943) and Coles (2001) are cited on the source page. Those books and papers were not opened. The general three-type theorem is logged rather than proved.

## Assumed
- The copies inside each finite-n calculation are independent and identically distributed.

## Disputed
- NIST's displayed Type I density is the law of a minimum. The article's Gumbel calculation is the limit of a maximum of exponential variables, which is the reflected form e^{−e^{−x}}. The name Type I is used only with that distinction in view.
