# Autocorrelation function — source notes

- Checked: Hyndman and Athanasopoulos (2021), *Forecasting: Principles and Practice*, 3rd ed., author-hosted §2.8 at https://otexts.com/fpptr/acf.html: the sample ACF formula, lag interpretation, correlogram, and seasonal peaks; §4.2 at https://otexts.com/fpptr/acf-features.html: ACF of differences can differ materially from ACF of levels. Existing bibliography key `hyndman_athanasopoulos2021`.
- Checked: The same authors' chapter 9, “ARIMA models,” at https://otexts.com/fpp3/arima.html: ACF and PACF patterns are tentative order-identification clues, trend can make the ACF decay slowly, and fitted residual ACF requires a separate diagnostic check.
- Checked: Dodge (2008), *The Concise Encyclopedia of Statistics*, printed pp. 24–25, “Autocorrelation”: basic lagged-correlation definition and historical pointer to Yule's work in the 1920s and 1930s. Its blanket statement that every time series ACF decays exponentially and its simple significance heuristic are not adopted; those claims need conditions.
- Checked: `sources/book/chapters/12-time-series.qmd`, weak-stationarity discussion: the population ACF is lagged covariance divided by variance when the mean and covariance are time-invariant. This inherited source text is retained by the builder.
- Checked: For the six-point alternating numerical example, exact arithmetic with Python `Fraction` gives mean $13/2$, denominator $35/2$, lag-one numerator $-25/4$, lag-two numerator $9$, and sample autocorrelations $-5/14$ and $18/35$. The proposed check script will assert these and every displayed decimal.
- Assumed: The six observations are an illustrative sequence, not evidence of a population seasonal process. Its empirical ACF is descriptive only.
- Disputed: A near-zero ACF does not establish independence, and a large ACF in levels may reflect a trend rather than stable serial dependence. The page will distinguish population and finite-sample ACFs and avoid a blanket confidence-band claim.
